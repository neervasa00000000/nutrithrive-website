Quick answer

  • Best fix: dried curry leaves are the same ingredient—use about 2–3× fresh volume and fry in hot oil.
  • True substitutes: kaffir lime leaf (about 1 leaf per 4–6 curry leaves) or lemon zest for citrus only.
  • Never: curry powder—it is a ground spice blend, not leaves. See curry leaves vs curry powder.

Why fresh curry leaves are hard to find

Supermarkets

Coles and Woolworths sometimes list fresh curry leaf punnets, but stock varies by store and week. Online listings are a hint, not a guarantee—check your local store or keep dried leaves in the pantry.

Indian and Asian grocers

Capital-city suburbs with strong South Asian grocery trade are the reliable source for fresh bunches tonight. Outside those areas, plan ahead or keep dried leaf in the pantry.

Backyard tree and freezer

If you have a tree, strip and freeze leaves flat in a bag—they fry well from frozen. Most Australian cooks do not have that option, which is why dried leaf matters for weeknight dal.

Swapping fresh for dried curry leaves

How many dried leaves?

Start with about double to triple the volume of fresh leaves in the recipe, then adjust by smell when they hit the oil. A typical dal for four might use 10–12 fresh leaves or about 20 good-quality dried leaflets.

How to cook dried leaves

Add them dry to hot ghee or oil at the tempering step—no soaking for tadka. Stir 5–20 seconds until fragrant and just darker at the edges; dried leaves scorch faster than fresh. Full technique: fresh vs dried curry leaves.

Substitutes that only sort of work

Bay leaves add minty backdrop, not karipatta citrus. Fresh basil can hint at anise in very mild curries; wrong for sambar. Fenugreek leaves suit some North Indian dals where methi already belongs. For a ranked list, see curry leaf substitute guide.

What not to use instead of curry leaves

Do not use curry powder as a leaf substitute—it changes the dish entirely. Skip turmeric alone, plain coriander, or generic “curry spice” jars. None reproduce leaf aroma in oil.

When to keep a dried pouch instead of substituting

Substitutes are for one dinner. They fail when curry leaf aroma is the point—sambar, rasam, coconut chutney, lemon rice. If you cook this way monthly or more, the substitute hunt gets old fast.

FAQ

What is the best substitute for fresh curry leaves?

Dried curry leaves if you can get them—they are the same ingredient. Next best is kaffir lime leaf, then lemon zest for citrus only.

Can I use curry powder instead of curry leaves?

No. Curry powder is a ground spice blend, not karipatta. See our curry leaves vs curry powder guide.

How many dried curry leaves equal fresh?

Use about 2–3 times the volume of dried leaves versus fresh, then adjust when they bloom in oil.

Can I get curry leaves at Coles?

Sometimes fresh in the herb section; dried is patchy. Stock varies by store. Asian grocers are better for fresh bunches; dried curry leaves online skip the hunt.

Can I skip curry leaves in a recipe?

Yes in a pinch—omit rather than force a poor swap in sambar, rasam or coconut chutney.

When should I buy dried instead of substituting?

When you cook with curry leaves often or substitutes keep failing the dish. Keep a sealed 30g pouch in the pantry.
