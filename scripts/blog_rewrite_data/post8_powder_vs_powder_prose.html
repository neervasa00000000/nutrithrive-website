Curry leaves and curry powder share a confusing English name. One is a whole leaf you fry in oil; the other is a ground spice blend for sauces and rubs. This guide compares them at a glance, explains when whole leaf beats powder, whether you can swap them (spoiler: no), how to make plain leaf powder and curry leaf podi at home, and where to buy real leaves in Australia.

Quick answer

If a recipe says curry leaves, buy the leaf—dried or fresh—and temper it in hot oil. Curry powder is a different ingredient (turmeric-forward blend) that almost never contains curry leaves. Do not substitute powder for leaves.

What curry leaf powder is (and is not)

Whole curry leaves can be dried and ground into a plain leaf powder for finishing rice or sprinkling on vegetables. That is still leaf, not the jar labelled “curry powder” in the supermarket spice aisle.

What curry powder is

Curry powder is a British-era blend meant to approximate “curry” flavour in one jar—turmeric for colour, cumin and coriander for body, chilli for heat. Useful for Anglo-Indian style sauces and quick weeknight “curry” when a recipe already expects ground spices.

Opening curry powder will not replace a tempering of whole karipatta—the volatile oils release in hot fat, not from a turmeric-heavy spoonful.

How to make powder from dried curry leaves

Plain dried leaf powder

  1. Toast a small handful of dried curry leaves in a dry pan on low heat until crisp and fragrant—1–2 minutes, do not scorch.
  2. Cool completely on a plate.
  3. Grind to a coarse powder in a spice grinder or mortar.
  4. Use within a few weeks; aroma fades faster than whole leaf.

Curry leaf podi (South Indian spice powder)

Podi blends roasted dals, chillies, cumin and dried curry leaves into a dry chutney powder for rice, idli and dosa. A typical batch uses about 10g dried curry leaves with chana dal, urad dal, dried chillies, cumin, pepper, salt and optional sesame, tamarind or jaggery—roast dals first, toast leaves last, cool, then grind coarse.

When whole leaf beats any powder

  • South Indian dal, sambar and rasam temperings
  • Lemon rice, coconut rice and upma finishes
  • Fish or prawn curries that start with mustard seed and karipatta
  • Curry leaf chutney and finishing oils

Can you swap curry powder for curry leaves?

No. Leaves give a citrus-savoury top note from whole-leaf tempering; curry powder gives body and colour. If you are out of leaves, do not tip in powder—use ranked swaps in our substitute guide until you restock dried curry leaves.

FAQ

Are curry leaves and curry powder the same?

No. Curry leaves are a whole aromatic leaf. Curry powder is a ground spice blend that almost never contains curry leaves.

Can I substitute curry powder for curry leaves?

No. They do different jobs. Use dried leaves when the recipe names the leaf.

How many dried curry leaves equal fresh?

Use about 2–3 times the volume of dried leaves versus fresh for a similar aroma in tadka.

What is curry leaf podi?

A roasted South Indian dry powder made with dals, chillies and dried curry leaves—served with rice or idli. See our podi recipe.
