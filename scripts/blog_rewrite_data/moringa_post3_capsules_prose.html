They are the same ingredient

Moringa capsules are gelatin or vegetable casings filled with ground moringa leaf — the same powder you would measure from a pouch. Tablets are another enclosed format. Searches for moringa powder vs capsules, moringa capsules or powder, and moringa powder vs tablets are really about dose maths and daily habit.

When capsules make sense

If you will not take moringa any other way — taste, texture, or routine — capsules can be the format you actually finish. You usually pay more per gram for that convenience. They also suit short trips where mixing powder is awkward.

When powder makes more sense

Powder suits anyone who wants to adjust the amount and mix into food they already eat. A typical teaspoon is roughly 2 to 3 g of leaf; capsule labels are often hundreds of milligrams per unit, so bottle maths matter.

Worked example (check the label on any bottle you compare): $40 for 60 capsules at 500 mg each is 30 g of leaf for $40 — about $1.33/g. NutriThrive at $14/100g is $0.14/g before shipping. Always divide pack price by total grams of moringa leaf.

How to compare cost fairly

  1. Find total grams of moringa leaf in the pack (capsule count × mg per capsule ÷ 1000, or net weight on a powder pouch).
  2. Divide price by grams, then multiply by 100 for $/100g.
  3. Compare like formats when you can — powder to powder, capsules to capsules.

What to check on any format

Format does not replace label and paperwork checks:

  • Ingredient list: 100% moringa leaf where possible.
  • Colour: bright green often points to shade-drying; dull brown can mean harsh heat or age (not a guarantee either way).
  • Pack or best-before date: so you know how long it has been packed.
  • Lab paperwork: can you open a summary for heavy metals or other tests? A badge alone is not a document.
