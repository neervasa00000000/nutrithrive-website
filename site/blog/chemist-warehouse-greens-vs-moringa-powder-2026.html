Label walk-through: blend vs one leaf

Vital is a blend. Spirulina is the largest slice at 24.8%. Then four plant powders at 17.4% each, chlorella at 5%, and three sweeteners under 1%. You are buying algae + brassicas + cereal grasses + sweetener stack. Organic certification covers the plant powders marked with an asterisk.

Mine is one leaf. If the bag says moringa, it is moringa. No proprietary blend line to decode. No sweetener to round off the green taste. If you hate the taste of leaf, you will notice. If you want it to taste like a flavoured drink, Vital's stevia / monk fruit / thaumatin stack is doing that job on purpose.

Storage after opening. Vital: refrigerate, use within 90 days. That is a short window for a 200 g tub if you travel or forget it at the back of the fridge. Mine: cool, dry pantry. Shelf-life guide on the site is measured in months, not a 90-day fridge clock: how long moringa powder lasts. Do not refrigerate my powder as a default; moisture is the enemy.

What "organic" does and does not buy you. Organic is a farming and processing certification. It is not a heavy-metals certificate by itself. It is not a TGA listing. It is not a disease claim. I am not certified organic. I publish an NMI batch summary for heavy metals and pesticide residues instead. Different proof. Different buyer.

The NMI PDF vs "trust the aisle"

On the moringa product page there is a link: View NMI Lab Summary (PDF). Example batch on that page: NT042024, Australian Government National Measurement Institute. It is a food-quality screen for heavy metals and pesticide residues. It is not a medical certificate. It is not an organic logo. It is not a cure.

How to read that kind of report: moringa heavy metals lab testing Australia. Ask any seller for a recent certificate of analysis before you buy, including me. Ask for the batch ID on your pack.

Pharmacy greens pages are often product copy. They rarely publish a government lab PDF next to the "Add to cart" button. That is one reason I bother packing in small runs and linking the summary on the PDP.

What greens powders will not do

I sell leaf. I do not sell a vegetable replacement.

The ABC dietitians' line is the one I use with customers: a powder is not a serve of broccoli. It will not fill you up like food. It will not fix a week of takeaway by itself. Today's grocery story has people swapping meat for lentils and buying less confectionery. That is pantry behaviour. A greens tub is optional.

Legal line I put on the bag and the site: food, not medicine. The TGA regulates therapeutic claims. I do not list AUST L numbers on moringa powder because I am not selling it as a listed medicine. Powder, leaves, and tea are legal to buy as food. Choose brands that publish batch testing rather than cure claims. I am not a doctor. ABN 32 639 442 616.

If a page says alkalise, cleanse, immunity, or detox as a reason to buy greens, treat that as marketing until a clinician you trust says otherwise. My FAQ and PDP say the same thing in fewer words.

How I actually use a teaspoon

Not a ritual. Not a shot glass of wellness.

  • Smoothie: ½, 1 tsp with banana or mango so the grass note is not the only flavour.
  • Yoghurt: ½ tsp stirred into plain yoghurt. Sweetener is your call; I do not put it in the bag.
  • Dal or soup: ½ tsp off the heat at the end so you do not cook it into sludge.
  • Skip it if you already ate a plate of actual greens. The powder is optional leaf, not a scoreboard.
