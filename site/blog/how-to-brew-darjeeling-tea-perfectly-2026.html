Quick Answer

Darjeeling is more sensitive to brew variables than most supermarket black teas. Wrong water temperature, a long steep, or too much leaf turns a floral cup harsh and flat. Water temperature for Darjeeling (just below boil)

Start at 85–90°C for first flush. A fuller second flush may suit 90–95°C. Full boiling water at 100°C pulls tannins hard from delicate Darjeeling leaf and makes the cup bitter and astringent — the usual reason people decide they “do not like Darjeeling.”

How to hit this without a fancy kettle:

  • Boil, then wait 45–90 seconds before pouring (first flush tends toward the longer wait).
  • Or boil, pour into a cool mug first, then onto the leaf.
  • Temperature-controlled kettles make this boringly consistent — useful if you brew daily.

Hard Melbourne tap water can mute aroma slightly. If your kettle scales quickly, a simple filter jug helps more than buying a fancier tin. Taste the first cup at the cooler end of the range, then nudge warmer next time within the flush band. Soft next step when the leaf is ready: Shop Darjeeling black tea.

Steep time — first flush vs second flush note

Flush is harvest timing. First flush (early spring) is lighter and more delicate. Second flush (early summer) is usually richer and more muscatel. Steep times should follow that, not a generic “5 minutes for all black tea” habit.

  • First flush: 2–3 minutes. Taste at 2 minutes; stop when aroma is clear and the cup is not drying on the tongue.
  • Second flush: 3–4 minutes. The fuller leaf can take a little longer without tipping into harshness as fast.

Going past about 4–5 minutes raises astringency regardless of temperature. Set a phone timer — the gap between 3 minutes and 5 minutes is obvious in the cup. Good whole leaf often takes a second, shorter steep; dust-grade bags usually do not. Deeper flush buying notes: first flush vs second flush.

Leaf-to-water ratio for Australian mugs/pots

Australian kitchen vessels vary. Use volume, not “one bag equals one serve” thinking.

  • Standard mug (~200–250ml): 1 level teaspoon loose leaf (~2g).
  • Large mug / keep-cup (~300–350ml): 1½ teaspoons.
  • Small teapot (~400–500ml, two cups): 2 teaspoons, shared.
  • Family pot (~700–800ml): about 1 teaspoon per cup you will pour, plus a pinch if you like a stronger first pour.

Do not heap extra leaf to chase strength — that adds bitterness faster than body. Prefer a slightly longer steep within the flush window, or a second short infusion. Milk or no milk?

Most Darjeeling drinkers start plain. Milk softens floral and muscatel notes and turns the cup toward a breakfast-style brew. That is preference, not a rule.

If you like milk in black tea, second flush usually handles a splash better than first flush — fuller body, less fragile aroma. Try the first cup black so you know what the leaf tastes like before you decide. English Breakfast habits do not automatically transfer; Darjeeling is a lighter, more aromatic style. FAQ

What temperature for Darjeeling?

Start at 85–90°C for first flush. A fuller second flush may suit 90–95°C. Avoid a rolling boil on the leaf.

How long should I steep first vs second flush?

First flush: about 2–3 minutes. Second flush: about 3–4 minutes. Past 4–5 minutes, astringency rises fast. Flush buying context: first flush vs second flush.

How much leaf for an Australian mug?

About one teaspoon (~2g) per 200–250ml mug. Scale up gently for keep-cups and teapots — more leaf adds bitterness faster than strength.

Milk or no milk?

Plain is the clearer tasting cup. Second flush usually handles a splash of milk better than first flush. Taste it black first.
