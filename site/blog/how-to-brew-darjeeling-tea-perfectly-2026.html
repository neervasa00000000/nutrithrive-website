How to Brew Darjeeling Tea: Temp, Time & Ratio (Loose Leaf)

Brew Darjeeling loose leaf right: 85–95°C water, 3–4 min, 2–2.5 g per cup. Milk or not, iced and pot ratios, and where to buy in Australia.

Quick brew

Use about 2 to 2.5 g of Darjeeling loose leaf (roughly one heaped teaspoon) per 200 ml cup. Heat water to about 85 to 95°C (just off the boil). Steep 3 to 4 minutes for a clear cup. Taste at 3 minutes; push toward 4 if you want more body. Skip a rolling boil for delicate first-flush style cups when you can.

Good Darjeeling is easy to brew and easy to spoil. The leaf already carries aroma; your job is not to “make it strong” the way English Breakfast bags teach you. Give the leaves space, keep the water just off the boil, and stop the timer before astringency takes over. Our loose leaf is $7.50 per 100g at /products/black-tea/.

Flush temperatures

First flush (spring) Darjeeling is often lighter and more delicate. Aim toward 85 to 90°C and the shorter end of the steep. Hotter water can push leafy astringency before the aroma has time to show. If your kettle only boils, pour into the cup or pot and wait about a minute before adding leaf.

Second flush usually has more body and the classic muscatel character. It can handle 90 to 95°C and a full 3 to 4 minutes without falling apart as quickly. This is the flush many Australian drinkers recognise as “Darjeeling” in the glass: grape-skin aroma, amber colour, and enough structure for a small splash of milk if you want it.

If your pouch does not name the flush, treat it as a mid-range Darjeeling black: start at about 90°C for 3 minutes, then adjust. Taste is the only lab that matters at home, if the cup is thin, add a little leaf next time; if it is harsh, drop temperature or time, not both at once. Flush differences are covered in more detail in our first flush vs second flush guide.

Hard Melbourne or regional tap water can mute aroma. If your tea tastes flat no matter how carefully you brew, try filtered water for one cup as a comparison. You are not chasing cafe theatre; you are checking whether the water, not the leaf, is the problem.

Leaf, pot and iced ratios

A clean starting point is about 1 g of leaf per 100 ml of water. Australian mugs are often 250 to 300 ml, so one “teaspoon habit” borrowed from a tiny English cup will underdose a big mug. Weigh once if you can; after that, a consistent heaped teaspoon is fine.

Use a small teapot or a wide infuser basket so leaves can open. Tiny cage infusers choke whole leaf and give a thin, harsh cup. For pots, preheat with hot water, brew, then pour everything out so the leaf does not keep stewing for the second pour.

For iced tea, prefer fridge cold brew overnight, or brew a slightly stronger hot cup and pour immediately over a full glass of ice. Leaving leaf in hot water for 8 to 10 minutes “to get strong enough for ice” is the fastest way to bitterness.

Milk or no milk

Try the first cup black so you know the leaf. If you like milk, second flush usually handles a splash better than a fragile first flush. English Breakfast habits do not always transfer; Darjeeling is a lighter, more aromatic black tea. Compare styles in Darjeeling vs English Breakfast.

If you add milk every time, brew a touch longer or use a gram more leaf so the cup does not taste watery. Do not jump straight to a rolling boil for five minutes; fix dose and time first.

Common mistakes

  • Rolling boil on delicate leaf, Wait 30 to 60 seconds off the boil, or use a variable kettle.
  • Oversteeping to “make it strong”, Strength comes from leaf amount more cleanly than from a six-minute punishment steep.
  • Tiny metal ball infusers, Leaves need room; use a basket or pot.
  • Squeezing the bag or pressing the leaf hard, You mostly press out bitterness.
  • Reboiling the same water all morning, Flat, over-oxygenated water tastes dull; use fresh water.
  • Judging Darjeeling by English Breakfast rules, Different body, different job.
  • Leaving leaf in the pot between pours, Decant fully, or the second cup stews.
  • Storing leaf next to spices or in a clear jar on a sunny shelf, Aroma fades; keep the pouch sealed, cool, and dark.

One more habit worth breaking: brewing “by colour” against an English Breakfast bag. Darjeeling often looks lighter in the cup even when the brew is finished. Trust the timer and the taste, not a dark mahogany target from another tea style.

FAQ

How long should I steep Darjeeling?

Start at 3 minutes; go to 4 if you want more body. Shorten the next steep if the cup turns harsh.

What temperature for Darjeeling tea?

About 85 to 95°C, just off the boil. First flush often prefers the cooler end.

How much leaf per cup?

About 2 to 2.5 g per 200 ml, or roughly 1 g per 100 ml for larger mugs.

Can I use milk in Darjeeling?

Yes. Try black first; second-flush style cups usually take milk better than delicate first flush.
