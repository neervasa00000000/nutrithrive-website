How to Brew Darjeeling Tea: Temp, Time & Ratio (Loose Leaf)

By Neer Vasa, NutriThrive founder · Updated 5 Oct 2026

Darjeeling is easy to brew well and easy to spoil. The leaf already carries aroma; your job is not to make it as strong as English Breakfast bags teach you. Below: a quick everyday brew, tea bags versus loose leaf, a brew table by method, water temperature, milk, common mistakes, and where to buy our loose leaf at $7.50 per 100g.

Quick brew

Use about 2 to 2.5g of Darjeeling loose leaf (roughly one heaped teaspoon) per 200ml cup. Heat water to about 85 to 95°C (just off the boil). Steep 3 to 4 minutes. Taste at 3 minutes; push toward 4 if you want more body. Skip a rolling boil for delicate first-flush style cups when you can.

Tea bags vs loose leaf for Darjeeling

Both can be black tea. Bags trade convenience for leaf quality and room to open. Darjeeling’s value is aroma; cramped bag dust often tastes flat or harsh if you oversteep to compensate.

Brewing with tea bags

If you only have a bag, use water just off the boil (not a rolling boil on delicate styles), steep 2 to 3 minutes, and do not squeeze the bag, you mostly press out bitterness. Bags are fine for travel; for everyday cups at home, loose leaf is worth the small extra step.

Brewing with loose leaf

Weigh once if you can: 2 to 2.5g per 200ml. Use a wide mug infuser or teapot basket so leaves open. One 100g pouch is about 40 to 50 cups at that dose. Compare styles in our Darjeeling vs English Breakfast guide.

Mug infusers, teapots and iced tea

Mug infuser

Pick a basket wide enough that wet leaf is not packed tight. Tiny metal balls give a thin, harsh cup. Australian mugs are often 250 to 300ml, so one teaspoon borrowed from a small cup may underdose, add a little leaf or use the large-mug row in the table.

Small teapot

Preheat with hot water, brew, then pour everything out for the first round so leaf does not keep stewing. Rule of thumb: about 1g leaf per 100ml water.

Iced and cold brew

Fridge cold brew overnight keeps bitterness lower than oversteeping hot tea onto ice. For flush differences and Melbourne tap notes, see first flush vs second flush in our buying guide.

Water temperature and flush

First flush (spring) Darjeeling is often lighter. Aim toward 85 to 90°C and the shorter end of the steep. If your kettle only boils, pour into the cup or pot and wait about a minute before adding leaf.

Second flush usually has more body and classic muscatel character. It can handle 90 to 95°C and a full 3 to 4 minutes.

Hard tap water can mute aroma. Try filtered water for one cup as a comparison before you blame the leaf.

Milk or no milk

Try the first cup black so you know the leaf. If you like milk, second flush usually handles a splash better than a fragile first flush. English Breakfast habits do not always transfer; fix dose and time before you jump to a five-minute boil. Compare in loose leaf black tea: Darjeeling vs English Breakfast.

Common mistakes

  • Rolling boil on delicate leaf, wait 30 to 60 seconds off the boil, or use a variable kettle.
  • Oversteeping to “make it strong”, strength comes from leaf amount more cleanly than from a six-minute steep.
  • Tiny ball infusers, leaves need room; use a basket or pot.
  • Squeezing bags or pressing wet leaf hard, you mostly press out bitterness.
  • Reboiling the same water all morning, flat water tastes dull; use fresh water.
  • Leaving leaf in the pot between pours, decant fully, or the second cup stews.
  • Storing leaf next to spices or in a sunny jar, keep the pouch sealed, cool and dark.

FAQ

How long should I steep Darjeeling?

Start at 3 minutes; go to 4 if you want more body. Shorten the next steep if the cup turns harsh.

What temperature for Darjeeling tea?

About 85 to 95°C, just off the boil. First flush often prefers the cooler end.

How much leaf per cup?

About 2 to 2.5g per 200ml, or roughly 1g per 100ml for larger mugs.

Can I use milk in Darjeeling?

Yes. Try black first; fuller style cups usually take milk better than very delicate first flush.
