Capsule vs powder — the shopping maths

Sticker price alone is a bad comparison. Format and grams in the pack matter.

Forest Super Foods at CW (verified 2 Sep 2026):

  • $79 for 120 × 500 mg capsules = 60 g of leaf powder inside the bottle
  • Per 100 g equivalent: about $131.67
  • At their 4-cap / 2000 mg serve: about $2.63 per serve, ~30 days if you use one serve daily

NutriThrive powder (live PDP):

  • $11 / 100 g → about $0.33 per daily 3 g serve (that's the PDP line)
  • $21.50 / 200 g and $35 / 400 g drop the per-100 g rate further
  • You scoop what you need into food you already eat — smoothie, yoghurt, soup, sauce

So you're not choosing "good brand vs bad brand" in a vacuum. You're choosing:

  1. Capsule convenience (pre-measured, no taste, familiar CW checkout) at a higher $/g, or
  2. Loose powder (cooking-friendly, adjustable spoon, lower $/g, public lab summary) from a small Melbourne packer

Neither format is "wrong". Capsules win when you travel or hate the grassy taste. Powder wins when you cook, want more leaf per dollar, and care about a downloadable batch summary.

Lab PDF vs "trust the aisle"

On my product page there's a link to the lab summary PDF. Example batch NT042024, National Measurement Institute report RN1453596. It's a food-quality summary for that batch — protein, fat profile, moisture, and the scope shown on the PDF. It is not a medical certificate. It is not an organic logo. It is not a therapeutic claim.

How to read reports in general: moringa heavy metals lab testing Australia. Ask any seller — including me — for a recent certificate before you buy. Ask for the batch ID on your pack.

Pharmacy capsule pages are often product copy. Some brands publish more elsewhere. On the CW listing I opened this morning, I didn't see a government lab PDF next to Add to cart. That's one reason I keep the summary on the PDP.
