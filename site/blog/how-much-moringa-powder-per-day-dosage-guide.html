Most adults do well on 1 to 2 level teaspoons of moringa powder a day, which is about 2.5 to 5 grams. Start with half a teaspoon for the first week, take it with food, and morning or lunchtime suits most people. There's little reason to go past 3 teaspoons, because that's where tummy upsets start to show up in the research.

This is one of the questions I get asked most. Someone opens their first pouch, looks at the bright green powder, and messages me: "Okay, how much do I actually put in?"

The pouch gives you the short answer. This post is the long one. I'll walk you through what the studies used, how I measure it at home, when to take it, how to stop it tasting like lawn clippings, and three recipes I make most weeks.

How much moringa powder per day is right for an adult?

For a healthy adult, 1 to 2 level teaspoons (roughly 2.5 to 5 g) a day is a sensible everyday amount.

That number isn't something I made up for the label. A 2025 study from California gave healthy adults one, two or three teaspoons of moringa leaf powder a day for a week. The researchers weighed it out: one teaspoon came to about 2.4 g, two to 4.8 g, and three to 7.2 g. People stuck with it at every dose, and they liked it about the same at every dose. The difference was in the gut. The three-teaspoon group reported more stomach symptoms than the one-teaspoon group. The authors noted those symptoms were mostly mild and short-lived, and that they tended to show up when people went above about 7 g a day (Matias et al., Preventive Medicine Reports, 2025).

A 2025 review that pooled nine controlled trials in adults (each lasting at least two weeks) found the more promising results came from doses under 10 g a day (Crișan et al., Nutrients, 2025). An earlier safety review of moringa found no adverse effects reported in the human studies that used powdered whole leaf (Stohs and Hartman, Phytotherapy Research, 2015).

So here's how I think about it:

Daily amountRoughlyWho it suits
½ teaspoon1.2 gYour first week, or if your stomach is sensitive
1 teaspoon2.4 gA good everyday amount for most people
2 teaspoons4.8 gIf you like the taste and it sits well with you
3 teaspoons7.2 gThe top end. More than this and your gut may complain

A quick note on spoons. These are level measuring teaspoons, the 5 mL kind from a baking set, not a heaped cutlery spoon. A heaped teaspoon of moringa can easily be double the amount, which is how people end up at 6 or 7 g without meaning to.

Should you start with a smaller dose?

Yes. I tell everyone to start with half a teaspoon a day for the first week.

Moringa is a dried leaf, so it's mostly fibre and plant matter. If your diet is light on greens, a big scoop on day one can mean bloating or a looser stomach for a day or two. That's the most common complaint I hear, and almost every time, the person started with a heaped tablespoon.

Week one: ½ teaspoon. Week two: 1 teaspoon. After that, stay at 1 or move up to 2 if you're enjoying it. Most people I talk to settle at one teaspoon and leave it there. If you want the full rundown on what too much looks like, I've written about moringa side effects and what happens if you take too much.

When should you take moringa powder: morning or night?

There's no study that shows a "best" time of day for moringa leaf powder. So pick the time you'll actually remember, and take it with food.

That said, here's what works well in practice:

  • Morning is the most common choice. It's easy to stir into a smoothie, yoghurt or juice at breakfast, and it becomes a habit fast.
  • Lunchtime is good if breakfast is just a coffee. Stir it into soup, dal or a salad dressing.
  • Night is fine too. Moringa leaf doesn't contain caffeine, so it won't keep you up the way a matcha would. Some people just find a heavy green drink before bed sits a bit odd.

One timing tip is worth knowing. Moringa leaf contains non-haem iron, the plant kind. Healthdirect, the Australian government's health information service, notes that tea and coffee can reduce iron absorption and that vitamin C foods like citrus, kiwi fruit, berries and capsicum can help (healthdirect, Foods high in iron). So if you're having moringa partly for the iron, have it with some fruit and keep it away from your morning flat white by an hour or so.

Taking it on an empty stomach isn't dangerous, but it's the most common way to get a queasy start. With food is the easy fix.

What's the best way to use moringa powder?

The golden rule: don't boil it to death. Moringa leaf is rich in vitamins and other plant compounds (Gopalakrishnan et al., Food Science and Human Wellness, 2016), and some of those don't love long, hot cooking. Stir it in at the end, or use it cold.

Easy places to hide a teaspoon:

  • Smoothies. The easiest option by a mile. Banana, mango or pineapple cover the taste.
  • Yoghurt or overnight oats. Stir in with honey and some berries.
  • Juice. Orange or apple juice, shaken in a jar. Bonus vitamin C.
  • Soups, dal and curries. Stir in after you take the pot off the heat.
  • Guacamole, pesto and hummus. It turns them a deeper green and nobody notices.
  • Salad dressing. Whisk into olive oil, lemon juice, salt and a little honey.
  • Scrambled eggs. Add once the eggs are almost set.

For baking, you can add a teaspoon to muffins or pancakes. You'll lose some of the more delicate nutrients in the oven, so I think of baking as a fun extra rather than my daily dose.

What does moringa taste like, and how do you make it taste better?

Fresh moringa powder tastes grassy and earthy, a bit like spinach or green tea, with a mild peppery finish. If it tastes dusty, flat or bitter, it may be old or badly dried. I go deeper into this in my guide to what moringa powder tastes like.

Tricks that help:

  1. Pair it with something sweet and sour. Banana, mango, pineapple, orange or a squeeze of lemon all work.
  2. Fat smooths the edges. Avocado, nut butter, coconut milk or Greek yoghurt round off the grassy note.
  3. Don't mix it into plain water. It clumps and tastes like a paddock. If you must, shake it in a jar with a little warm water first to make a paste, then top up.
  4. Ginger and mint are friends. Both play nicely with the peppery side.
  5. Start small. Half a teaspoon in a big smoothie is barely noticeable. Your taste buds get used to it.

Three recipes I make most weeks

1. Morning green smoothie (1 teaspoon)

  • 1 frozen banana
  • ½ cup frozen mango or pineapple
  • 1 cup milk of your choice
  • 1 tablespoon peanut butter
  • 1 level teaspoon moringa powder
  • A few ice cubes

Blend for 30 seconds. This is how most people start, and the fruit hides the moringa completely. If you want more ideas like this, there's a whole post of moringa smoothie recipes.

2. Moringa lemon ginger shot (½ teaspoon)

  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • 1 cm fresh ginger, finely grated
  • 1 teaspoon honey
  • ½ level teaspoon moringa powder
  • 60 mL warm (not hot) water

Shake everything in a small jar and drink it straight. Lemon brings vitamin C. Warm water helps the honey dissolve without cooking the moringa.

3. Moringa dal finish (1 teaspoon per serve)

Make your usual dal. When it's done, take the pot off the heat, then stir in 1 level teaspoon of moringa per serve along with a squeeze of lemon. It's how we use moringa at home, and the easiest way to get that flavour from a pouch. More everyday ideas are in 12 ways to add moringa to food you already cook.

Is moringa powder better than capsules for getting the dose right?

Powder is easier to adjust, because you can go up or down by half a teaspoon. Check the label on any capsule product for how many milligrams each one holds, since a single level teaspoon of powder is about 2.4 g. Capsules make sense if you hate the taste or travel a lot. For most people, a teaspoon in food is simpler. For the cost side, see moringa capsules vs powder.

Who should check with a doctor first?

Please talk to your GP or pharmacist before taking moringa if you're pregnant or breastfeeding, on regular medicines (especially for blood sugar, blood pressure or thyroid, or blood thinners), or managing a health condition. The same goes for children, who need much smaller amounts.

FAQ

How many teaspoons of moringa powder should I take a day?

Most adults do well on 1 to 2 level teaspoons a day, about 2.5 to 5 g. Start with half a teaspoon for the first week. In a 2025 study, people taking three teaspoons (about 7 g) reported more stomach symptoms than those taking one.

Is it better to take moringa in the morning or at night?

No study shows a best time. Take it with food at whatever meal you'll remember. Morning works for most people. Moringa leaf has no caffeine, so night is fine too.

Can I take moringa on an empty stomach?

You can, but it's the most common cause of a queasy or unsettled stomach. Having it with food, even a piece of fruit or some yoghurt, usually avoids that.

How long does a 100 g pouch of moringa powder last?

About 40 level teaspoons, so roughly 5 to 6 weeks at one teaspoon a day, or 3 weeks at two. Once opened, use it within a year and keep it sealed, cool and dark.

Can I put moringa powder in hot tea or coffee?

You can stir it into warm drinks, but let them cool for a minute first rather than pouring on boiling water. Keep it away from coffee and black tea if iron is one of your reasons for taking it, since both can reduce iron absorption.

This is general information only. Please check with your GP if you have a health condition, take medicines, or are pregnant or breastfeeding.
