Coles, Woolworths and Aldi: what we found

Moringa powder at Coles

Coles showed no moringa powder online on 9 November 2026. We searched Coles online for "moringa powder" and "moringa". No loose leaf powder, no capsules, and no moringa tea came up as a Coles own-brand or Coles-stocked item.

That does not mean Coles will never stock it. It means, on the day we checked, you could not buy moringa powder at Coles.

Moringa powder at Woolworths

Woolworths listed about a dozen Everyday Market powders on 9 November 2026, at about $5.86 to $18.75 per 100g. Everyday Market is Woolworths' marketplace. The powders are sold and shipped by third-party sellers, not by Woolworths itself.

Before you buy one, check three things: who the seller is, where it ships from, and what the total delivered price is, including shipping. A low price per 100g can become expensive once shipping is added, and a marketplace listing does not always show a lab summary.

Moringa powder at Aldi

Aldi showed no moringa powder online on 9 November 2026. Aldi's range changes with its Special Buys, so it is worth checking again, but on the day we checked, there was nothing to buy.

Chemist Warehouse: capsules, not powder

Chemist Warehouse mainly lists moringa capsules, not loose leaf powder. On 9 November 2026, the main listing was Forest Super Foods moringa capsules, 120 capsules at $79.

Each capsule is 500mg, so 120 capsules is 60g of leaf. That works out to about $132 per 100g of leaf.

Chemist Warehouse also lists moringa tablets and greens blends, but those are not the same as plain leaf powder. A greens blend usually contains moringa as one ingredient among many, so the price per 100g of moringa leaf is not comparable.

If you want capsules from a pharmacy you trust, Chemist Warehouse is a straightforward option. If you want loose leaf powder by weight, you will need to buy it elsewhere.

Amazon AU and iHerb: what to check

Amazon AU and iHerb both list moringa powder, but the seller, the origin, and the price per 100g change from listing to listing.

Check these four things before you buy from a marketplace:

  1. Who is the seller? Is it the brand, or a reseller? Is there an Australian ABN and address you can look up?
  2. What is in the pouch? The label should read "moringa oleifera leaf powder" as a single ingredient. No fillers, no blends, unless the blend is clearly labelled.
  3. Price per 100g, delivered. Divide the total price, including shipping, by the grams of leaf in the pack, then multiply by 100. That is the only fair comparison.
  4. Lab testing. Is there a lab summary for the batch, from an Australian lab, that you can read? If not, you are buying on trust alone.

A cheap pouch from an unknown seller, with no lab summary and no Australian address, is not the same as a cheap pouch from a farm packer who publishes their testing.

Rosabella and other overseas brands: the delivered total matters

On 9 November 2026, its Australian store showed $52 for one 60-capsule bottle, 24g of leaf, about $217 per 100g. Orders ship from Ohio, USA, and the buyer pays any customs duties, taxes and import fees.

That is not a criticism of Rosabella as a company. It is a reminder to compare the delivered total, not just the headline price. Check the shipping quote and return address before paying.

The same rule applies to any overseas brand: product price + shipping + import charges, divided by grams of leaf, is the real price per 100g.

What to check before you buy any moringa powder

Use this checklist, whoever you buy from:

  • One ingredient: "moringa oleifera leaf powder", nothing else, unless a blend is clearly labelled.
  • Gram weight: the pack should state the weight in grams, not just "60 capsules" or "30 serves".
  • Price per 100g, delivered: total price including shipping, divided by grams, times 100.
  • Lab summary: an Australian lab summary for the batch, not just "lab tested" on the label.
  • Australian address and ABN: if it is an Australian business, you should be able to look it up.
  • Returns: a clear window, a return address, and who pays postage.
  • Best-before date: the pack should carry a date, and the powder should last 1 year once opened if kept sealed, cool and dry.

If a listing cannot answer those seven points, compare it with one that can.
