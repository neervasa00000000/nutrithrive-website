Quick answer

Put 1 tablespoon of dried curry leaves in about 500 ml of water. Heat until small bubbles appear at the edge, then simmer gently for 5 to 8 minutes. Strain, then drink warm. Lemon and honey make the first cup easier to enjoy.

Curry leaf tea is a quiet habit in many South Indian kitchens: not flashy, just leaves and water. If you live outside the suburbs where fresh karipatta shows up at the greengrocer every week, dried leaves are the practical way to try it. We grow and shade-dry curry leaves on our farm and pack them in Truganina; the pouch is $7 for 30g.

How to make it

What you need

  • 1 tbsp dried curry leaves (about 2 to 3 g)
  • 500 ml water (roughly two mugs)
  • Optional: half a lemon, honey, or a thin slice of fresh ginger

Steps

  1. Add the dried leaves and water to a small saucepan.
  2. Bring to a gentle simmer. A hard rolling boil can make the cup taste flat.
  3. Simmer for 5 to 8 minutes until the liquid turns pale yellow-green.
  4. Strain into a mug. Compost or discard the spent leaves.
  5. Stir in lemon and honey if you like. Ginger can go in at step 1 if you want a warmer note.

That batch is one large mug or two smaller cups. The leaves do not infuse well a second time, so start fresh next round.

Steeping instead of simmering: You can pour just-boiled water over 1 tbsp dried leaves in a teapot, cover, and steep 8 to 10 minutes. The flavour is softer; simmering pulls more out of dried leaf.

What does it taste like?

Think mild, savoury, and a little citrusy underneath. It is not sweet on its own, and it is not like black tea. The aroma you know from tadka is there, but quieter. Most people prefer lemon and honey the first few times. If you normally drink builder’s tea, adjust your expectations: this is an herbal cup, not a caffeine hit.

How much leaf to use

One tablespoon per 500 ml is a good starting point. Like stronger flavour? Use a heaped spoon or simmer a minute longer. Too bitter? Use slightly less leaf or shorten the simmer. Dried leaf is concentrated compared with fresh, so you do not need a handful.

Keep the pouch sealed and out of direct sun. For storage tips, see how to store curry leaves.

FAQ

Can you make tea from dried curry leaves?

Yes. Dried leaves are what most Australians use, because fresh karipatta is not always in the shops.

Do you need fresh leaves?

No. This guide is written for dried leaf. Fresh leaves work too, but they are harder to source consistently outside capital cities.

How long should you simmer?

Five to eight minutes at a gentle simmer is enough for one tablespoon in 500 ml water.

What goes well with it?

Lemon, honey, and a small piece of ginger are the usual additions. Drink it on its own if you already like herbal infusions.
