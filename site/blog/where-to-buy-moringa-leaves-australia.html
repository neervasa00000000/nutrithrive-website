Moringa, drumstick, malunggay: one leaf, many names

Moringa leaves, drumstick leaves and malunggay are the same thing: the leaves of Moringa oleifera. “Drumstick” comes from the long seed pods. Because shops label the leaf in different languages, searching under one name can make it look out of stock when it is sitting on the shelf under another. The University of Melbourne’s Multilingual Multiscript Plant Name Database lists these common names:

Where to buy fresh moringa leaves

Moringa is a warm-climate tree. Our moringa growing guide explains why it often drops its leaves through winter in subtropical and cooler zones. That is why fresh sprigs are easiest to find in warmer parts of Australia and through people who grow their own, and why they come and go in Melbourne, Adelaide and Hobart.

In the capital cities, ask at:

  • Filipino grocers: ask for malunggay. It is a standard leaf in tinola, so it is worth asking there first.
  • Tamil, Sri Lankan and South Indian grocers: ask for murungai keerai or murunga kolle.
  • North Indian grocers: ask for sahjan leaves or drumstick leaves. Some stock the pods rather than the leaves, so ask for leaves specifically.
  • Local growers: community groups and neighbourhood marketplaces in warmer suburbs sometimes have people selling or swapping sprigs from backyard trees.

“Moringa leaves near me”: a quick checklist

  1. Call before you drive. Fresh stock may not arrive every week.
  2. Ask using at least two names (for example malunggay and drumstick leaves).
  3. Choose bright green leaflets. Skip bunches that are yellowing or dropping leaves in the bag.
  4. At home, wrap the stems loosely in paper towel, keep them in a container in the fridge, and cook within a couple of days.
  5. Strip the leaflets off the stems just before cooking. The thicker stems are woody.

Frozen moringa: pods vs leaves

Search “frozen moringa” or “frozen drumstick” and most of what comes up is cut drumstick pods. These are the long green sticks that go into sambar. For example, one Australian frozen-vegetable brand sells a 312g pack of pre-cut drumstick pods through Indian grocery stores. Pods are a different ingredient from the leaves, with a different texture and use. If the recipe calls for leaves, read the front of the pack before you buy. If you cannot find frozen leaves, dried leaves or powder are the practical stand-ins.

Dried moringa leaves

Dried leaves are the easiest whole-leaf format to buy in Australia. When we checked online Indian grocery listings on 7 Oct 2026, dried “drumstick leaves” or “moringa leaves” came in small packs of about 25g to 100g, and some online sellers offer 1kg bags of whole dried leaves.

How to use them:

  • Crumble at the end. Rub a small handful between your palms over dal, rasam or soup in the last minute of cooking.
  • Sift out stems. Dried packs often include bits of stem. Shake the leaves in a sieve first.
  • Grind your own. A spice grinder turns dried leaves into a coarse powder for podi or dough.
  • Store dry and dark. Seal the pack after opening and keep it away from steam and sunlight.

Moringa leaf powder: the year-round swap

Fresh moringa leaves are mostly water. USDA FoodData Central lists raw drumstick leaves at 78.66 g of water per 100 g, and it gives a chopped cup of fresh leaves as 21 g. Once the water is gone, a loose cupful of fresh leaves shrinks to a small spoonful of dry leaf. That is why recipes need far less powder than fresh leaves, and why powder tastes stronger spoon for spoon.

These swaps are starting points. Taste, then adjust.

Buying guide: how to choose dried leaves or powder

Whether you buy a 25g pack of dried leaves from a grocer or a pouch of powder online, the same checks apply.

  1. Name and plant part: the label should say moringa leaf or Moringa oleifera leaf, not seed or a vague “greens blend”.
  2. One ingredient: 100% moringa leaf. Blends are a different product.
  3. Colour: bright to medium green. Dull khaki usually means older stock or harsh drying. See shade-dried vs sun-dried moringa.
  4. Date: a packed-on date or batch code you can actually read.
  5. A size you will finish: open packs are best used while they still smell fresh and green. Our moringa shelf-life guide covers storage.
  6. Price per 100g: divide the price by the grams, then multiply by 100. It is the only fair way to compare a 25g pack with a 400g pouch.
  7. Paperwork: ask whether the seller can show a lab summary for the batch. Ours is the NMI lab summary (PDF).
  8. Dispatch: check where it ships from and how long it takes to reach you.

FAQ

Are moringa leaves and drumstick leaves the same?

Yes. Both names refer to the leaves of the same tree, Moringa oleifera. “Drumstick” comes from the long seed pods, which are also sold as a vegetable.

Is malunggay the same as moringa?

Yes. Malunggay is the Tagalog (Filipino) name for Moringa oleifera. Malunggay leaves, moringa leaves and drumstick leaves are the same leaf.

Does Woolworths sell moringa leaves?

Not fresh or frozen leaves, based on our 7 Oct 2026 search. The 17 “moringa leaves” results were third-party Everyday Market listings for leaf powder, capsules and oils. Stock changes, so check the Woolworths app on the day.

Can I use moringa powder instead of fresh malunggay in tinola?

Yes, for flavour and colour. Start with 1 teaspoon of powder where the recipe calls for about a cup of fresh leaves. Stir it in off the heat at the end, taste, and add more if you like. You will not get the soft leaf texture.

How do you use dried moringa leaves?

Sift out any stems, then crumble a small handful into dal, rasam or soup in the last minute of cooking. You can also grind dried leaves in a spice grinder to use like powder.

Can you buy frozen moringa leaves in Australia?

The frozen moringa packs we found at Indian grocers were drumstick pods, not leaves. Read the front of the pack. If you cannot find frozen leaves, dried leaves or leaf powder are the practical stand-ins.

Where can I buy moringa leaves in Melbourne?

For fresh sprigs, ask at Filipino, Tamil, Sri Lankan and South Indian grocers, using several names, and call ahead. Dried leaves are sold at Indian grocers. Our moringa leaf powder ships from Truganina, and local pickup can be arranged.
