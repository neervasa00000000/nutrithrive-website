Finding a present for a tea lover is harder than it sounds. They usually have a favourite mug, a cupboard of half-finished boxes and strong opinions. My approach is simple: give them one good loose leaf tea they might not buy for themselves, plus something small that makes brewing it easier. Here are tea gift set ideas by budget, how to build your own small tea hamper, and the ready-made gift box we pack in Melbourne.

What makes a good present for a tea lover

Tea people tend to like three things in a gift:

  1. A tea with a story they can taste. A single-origin tea such as Darjeeling, from the hills of West Bengal, is more interesting to try than another box of supermarket bags.
  2. Loose leaf. Many tea drinkers mean to try loose leaf and never get round to it. A pouch plus an infuser removes the excuse.
  3. Something they'll use up. Tea gets drunk. That's the point: it doesn't add to the clutter.

What they don't need is a novelty tin with a tiny amount of tea, or a blend so heavily flavoured they won't finish it.

Build your own tea gift set or small tea hamper

A tea hamper doesn't need a giant basket full of fillers. Three good things in a small box or a tea towel tied with string looks better and is easier to post.

The tea

Start with one loose leaf tea, 100g. Darjeeling is a good gift tea because it suits people who drink their tea black as well as people who like a splash of milk. If you know they love strong breakfast tea, add a note suggesting they try it with a little milk first.

Something to brew it with

A wide mug infuser, a small teapot with a built-in strainer, or a pack of paper tea filters. Any of these turns a pouch of leaf into a cup without fuss. A tea towel or a nice mug works too.

The card

This is the part people remember. Write the brew on it: "2 to 2.5g (one heaped teaspoon) per cup, water at 85 to 90°C, steep 3 to 4 minutes. Try it black first." Add a line about why you chose it.

Packing tip: if your hamper includes soap or anything scented, wrap it separately so the tea doesn't pick up the smell.

Giving Darjeeling: what to tell the person you're giving it to

If they're used to tea bags, a few pointers on your card will help them enjoy it:

  • Don't use boiling water. About 85 to 90°C (let the kettle sit for a minute after it boils) keeps Darjeeling from tasting harsh.
  • Weigh once. About 2 to 2.5g per 200ml cup. After that, a heaped teaspoon is fine.
  • Try it black first. Then add milk if they like.
  • Store it well. Keep the pouch sealed, cool and dark, away from spices and the stove.

It contains caffeine, about 40 to 50mg per cup, so it's a daytime tea for most people. Our full Darjeeling brewing guide covers teapots, iced tea and milk. To compare it with other black teas, see loose leaf black tea: Darjeeling vs English Breakfast.
