Quick answer

A typical mug of Darjeeling black tea sits in a lower band than drip coffee. Coffee often runs roughly twice the caffeine of a standard Darjeeling cup, sometimes more, depending on how you brew each one.

If you are weighing Darjeeling against your morning coffee, you are usually choosing a lighter caffeine load, not zero caffeine. Darjeeling is still black tea. We source ours from a family farm in Darjeeling and pack loose leaf in Truganina; 100g is $7.50.

Tea vs coffee

Exact numbers shift with leaf amount, water volume, and steep time. These ranges are the usual ballpark for one mug:

Drink Caffeine per cup (typical range)
Drip or plunger coffee About 95 to 200 mg
Single espresso About 60 to 100 mg
Darjeeling black tea About 30 to 60 mg
Strong Assam or English Breakfast About 40 to 70 mg

Darjeeling is grown high in the hills, and the leaves are often lighter in body than lowland Assam. That is one reason many cups taste delicate and sit toward the middle or lower end of the tea row, not the top.

First flush (spring) Darjeeling can skew lighter; second flush can feel fuller and sometimes a touch stronger in the cup. For flush differences, see our first flush vs second flush guide.

What changes your cup

Steep time is the lever you feel most. Caffeine dissolves quickly in hot water. A three-minute steep is not the same as six minutes with the same leaf.

Leaf amount matters too. More leaf in the infuser means more caffeine, full stop.

Water temperature changes flavour more than it changes caffeine, but boiling hard and long can extract slightly more overall.

If you want a gentler cup, use a little less leaf or stop the timer earlier. For brewing temps and times, read how to brew Darjeeling tea.

Stepping down from coffee

Darjeeling is a realistic halfway point if you like a warm ritual but want less caffeine than a double-shot latte habit. You still get alertness; you do not get coffee’s intensity or the same jitter profile for most people.

The taste is different: muscatel notes, lighter body, no milk required (though you can add milk if you prefer). Try it for a week before you judge it as a replacement.

FAQ

Does Darjeeling have less caffeine than coffee?

Usually yes. Most home-brewed coffee cups exceed a typical Darjeeling mug.

Is Darjeeling caffeine-free?

No. It is real black tea. Decaf Darjeeling exists elsewhere; our product is regular loose leaf.

Can I drink Darjeeling in the afternoon?

That is personal. It still contains caffeine. If late cups disturb your sleep, drink it earlier or use less leaf and a shorter steep.

How much leaf should I use?

About one teaspoon per mug is a common starting point for loose leaf. Adjust to taste.
