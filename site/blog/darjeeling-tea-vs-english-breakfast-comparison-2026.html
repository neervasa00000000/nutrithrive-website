Darjeeling vs Assam, Ceylon and Earl Grey

Darjeeling is typically lighter than a robust Assam. Ceylon styles vary by growing region. Earl Grey describes tea flavoured with bergamot rather than a growing region, so check the base tea on the label.

Why loose leaf black tea beats bags for flavour

Whole or broken loose leaf has room to open in the water. Tea bags often use smaller particles that extract faster and can taste flat or harsh if you oversteep. Loose leaf also lets you weigh the dose once, then use a heaped teaspoon habit after that.

For Darjeeling, the difference matters because the cup is about aroma, muscatel, floral notes, not just colour. Bags exist, but they lose that top note quickly. If you are stepping up from supermarket English Breakfast bags, loose leaf Darjeeling is the upgrade that actually tastes different, not just “stronger tea.”

Four black teas compared

Shoppers often treat the black-tea aisle as one product. These four styles do different jobs in an Australian kitchen.

Darjeeling

High-grown tea from the Darjeeling hills in West Bengal. Lighter body, floral or grape-skin aroma, optional milk. Best when you want to taste the leaf itself.

English Breakfast

Usually a blend, often Assam-forward, designed for milk and toast. Malty, robust, near-boiling water. Keep it for the ritual where milk is non-negotiable every morning.

Assam

Lowland Indian black tea: maltier and stronger than Darjeeling. Often the backbone of breakfast blends. Choose Assam or Assam-heavy blends when you want colour and punch with milk.

Ceylon

Sri Lankan black tea, often bright and brisk. Good everyday tea; less floral-muscatel than Darjeeling and less heavy than Assam.

Which tea to buy

If you always drink tea with milk

English Breakfast or Assam-forward blends are built for that job. Darjeeling can take a small splash, especially fuller second-flush style cups, but it is not trying to imitate a builder’s mug. Do not force one tea to do every job poorly, many kitchens keep a strong breakfast blend for milk days and a Darjeeling pouch for black or lightly milky cups.

If you prefer black tea without milk

Darjeeling is the obvious loose leaf pick: interesting aroma, cleaner finish, lower bitterness when you use water just off the boil. Ceylon is a good second everyday tea if you like brisk rather than floral.

If you are buying a gift

Darjeeling suits tea drinkers who might not buy single-origin leaf for themselves. Pair 100g with a handwritten brew card (85 to 90°C, 3 to 4 minutes). Our tea gift ideas cover hampers under $20 and the ready-made gift box.

How to brew loose leaf Darjeeling

Use about 2 to 2.5g of leaf per 200ml cup (roughly one heaped teaspoon). Heat water to 85 to 90°C, let the kettle sit a minute after boiling. Steep 3 to 4 minutes, taste at three, stop at four if you want more body without harshness.

Use a wide mug infuser or small teapot so leaves can open. Avoid tiny ball infusers that choke the leaf. Store the pouch sealed, cool and dark, away from spices and the stove.

FAQ

What is the best loose leaf black tea?

It depends on the cup you want. Darjeeling is best for an aromatic black tea you can drink without milk. English Breakfast or Assam-heavy blends are best for strong milk tea. We sell Darjeeling loose leaf at $7.50 per 100g.

Is Darjeeling the same as English Breakfast?

No. Both can be black tea, but Darjeeling is a regional style valued for aroma and a lighter finish. English Breakfast is usually a stronger blend designed for milk.

Should I put milk in Darjeeling?

Try it black first. A splash of milk works better with fuller cups than with very delicate first-flush style tea.

How much caffeine is in Darjeeling?

Typical home brews sit around 30 to 60 mg per mug, depending on leaf dose, mug size and steep time. Neither Darjeeling nor English Breakfast is caffeine-free.

How much leaf per cup?

About 2 to 2.5g per 200ml, or roughly 1g per 100ml if you scale up for a large mug.
