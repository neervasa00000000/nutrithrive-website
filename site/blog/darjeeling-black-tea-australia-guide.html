What Darjeeling is (region and flavour)

Darjeeling tea is grown in the Darjeeling district of West Bengal, India, in the Himalayan foothills. It’s still Camellia sinensis; the difference is place and processing. Expect a lighter body than Assam-heavy breakfast blends, with floral or muscatel notes when the leaf is fresh and the water isn’t boiling. Prefer packs that say Darjeeling clearly. “Darjeeling style” blends are a different product.

First flush vs second flush (short)

First flush (spring) tends to be lighter and more delicate. Second flush (early summer) is usually richer and closer to what people mean by classic Darjeeling.

How to brew (short; full guide linked)

Use water at about 85 to 90°C, 2 to 2.5g of leaf per cup, and steep 3 to 4 minutes. Taste at 3 minutes. Try the first cup black. Full teapot, milk and iced notes are in how to brew Darjeeling tea perfectly.

Darjeeling vs English Breakfast (short)

English Breakfast is usually a stronger blend built for milk and toast. Darjeeling is a regional tea with more aroma and less malt punch. Neither is “better”; they suit different mornings. Full side-by-side: Darjeeling vs English Breakfast.

How to store Darjeeling in Australia

Keep loose leaf airtight, cool and dark, away from the oven and the spice rack. Don’t fridge it, because condensation and food smells are worse than a cupboard. Opened packs taste brightest for months; when the cup smells of cardboard, the leaf is tired.

Hard tap water can mute aroma slightly; if your kettle scales quickly, a simple filter jug helps more than buying a fancier pouch. Rinse yesterday’s milky film from the cup before tasting a plain cup, because leftover milk fat changes how you judge the leaf.
