Fresh, dried or frozen: which one to buy

  • Fresh punnet (Coles, Woolworths): the brightest aroma. They wilt within days, so they suit cooking this week.
  • Frozen sprigs (if your grocer has a freezer pack): close to fresh. Fry them straight from frozen.
  • Dried whole leaves: keep in the cupboard for months, so they’re always there for weeknight dal, sambar, rasam or lemon rice. The aroma is softer than fresh, so use a few more leaves.

Lots of cooks keep a pouch of dried leaves and buy fresh when a good punnet turns up.

What to look for in a pack of dried curry leaves

  • Whole leaflets, not grey-green dust.
  • Colour: green to olive. Uniform brown usually means old stock.
  • Smell: crush one leaf. It should smell warm and citrusy, not like cardboard.
  • A date you can read, and a pouch that seals again.
  • Ingredient list: just curry leaves (Murraya koenigii). It isn’t curry powder, which is a spice blend.

How to use dried curry leaves (short)

Heat 1 to 2 tablespoons of oil or ghee until a mustard seed sizzles straight away. Add mustard or cumin seeds, then 8 to 15 dried curry leaves for a pot of dal for 3 to 4 people. They should darken a shade within 10 to 20 seconds. Pour the tadka over the dal. Don’t soak the leaves first, and don’t fry them until they turn black, because dried leaves scorch faster than fresh.

How to store them

Keep the pouch sealed in a cool, dark cupboard away from the stove and the kettle’s steam. Dried leaves don’t spoil quickly when they’re kept dry, but the aroma fades, so finish an open pouch within a year. If a pack ever smells musty or feels damp, throw it out.

Are dried curry leaves as good as fresh?
