Fresh punnets sit in the refrigerated herb section. Dried leaves, where stocked, are with the dried herbs and spices. If your store is out, Indian and Sri Lankan grocers usually have stems or larger dried packs, and you can buy dried curry leaves online for a pouch that lasts months.

Fresh or dried: which one to buy

Buy fresh if you'll cook with them within a week. Keep them in the fridge, wrapped in paper towel, and use them soon, because they wilt quickly in humid weather.

Buy dried if you want curry leaves on hand every week without a special trip. Whole dried leaflets live next to mustard seeds and cumin, and they wake up in hot oil for tadka. Many people who cook South Indian or Sri Lankan food keep both: fresh when the punnet looks good, dried for weeknight dal.

Indian grocers and buying dried curry leaves online

Indian and Sri Lankan grocers still matter for bulk fresh stems and large dried packs. When you open a dried pack, look for:

  • Whole leaflets, not grey-green powder that smells of cardboard
  • A clear pack date or batch
  • Colour that still looks green-olive, not uniform brown dust
  • An airtight pouch that still feels crisp

How to use dried curry leaves in tadka (short)

Dried leaves need hot fat. In a small pan, heat 1 to 2 tablespoons of oil or ghee, pop mustard seeds, then add a small handful of dried curry leaves for 10 to 20 seconds until they darken a shade and smell strong. Pour the lot over dal, sambar or vegetables. Don't soak the leaves first, and don't leave them in the pan until they turn black, because dried leaves scorch faster than fresh ones.

How to store dried curry leaves

Keep them airtight, cool and dark, away from the stove. A glass jar with a tight lid works well once the pouch is open. Opened packs stay useful for months if kept airtight; replace when the leaves smell of dust or look pale brown.

Light and kitchen heat age dried herbs the same way they age paprika, so don't leave the open pouch beside the cooktop. Scoop with a dry spoon, because a wet spoon from a rinsed mug is a quiet way to spoil a jar. If you buy a larger pack than you'll finish soon, split it: a small jar for daily cooking and a sealed backup in the dark cupboard.
