How to compare moringa brands in Australia

The Australian moringa shelf has grown fast. Online brands, pharmacy listings, and health-store pouches all claim premium leaf. Most roundups hand out a trophy without showing the scorecard. This page is a brand comparison: how to judge pouches fairly, a short look at brands shoppers ask about, then the powder we sell at NutriThrive.

We sell moringa powder, so we have a commercial interest. We apply the same checklist to our pouch as to anyone else. This is not a Chemist Warehouse aisle walkthrough, not a Rosabella review, not a capsules-versus-powder essay, and not the full eight-point quality checklist (that lives on its own page).

The five-point brand checklist

Run every brand you consider through these five questions:

  1. Lab testing: can you open a Certificate of Analysis or lab summary that shows heavy metals results for the lot you might buy? A "tested" badge with no document is not enough.
  2. Single-ingredient label: is the list 100% moringa leaf? Fillers and blends make dose maths harder.
  3. Processing: shade-dried leaf tends to stay bright green. Dull or brown powder often means harsh drying or age.
  4. Pack date: is there a harvest or pack date you can read?
  5. Price per 100g: normalise to $/100g so a 100g pouch and a 500g bag can be compared fairly.
