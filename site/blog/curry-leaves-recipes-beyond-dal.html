Dal is the obvious home for curry leaves, but the same 60-second tadka lifts lemon rice, chutney, eggs, potatoes and green beans. Below: the quick tempering method, six recipes that use it, how many dried leaves per dish, fresh versus dried, and keeping a pantry pouch so you are never mid-recipe without karipatta.

In Australia, fresh curry leaves at Woolworths and Coles come in small punnets and stock varies by store, so every recipe below works with dried leaves from the pantry. See where to buy curry leaves in Australia.

Quick answer

Fry 8 to 15 dried curry leaves in 1 to 2 tbsp hot oil or ghee for 30 to 60 seconds until fragrant, then build the dish or pour over rice or dal. Drying softens the aroma, so use a little more than you would fresh. A 30g pouch covers weeks of weekday cooking when resealed and kept cool.

Tadka in 60 seconds

  1. Heat 1 to 2 tbsp oil or ghee until it shimmers.
  2. Add mustard seeds if the recipe uses them; let them pop.
  3. Add dried curry leaves, they should sizzle.
  4. Optional: dried red chilli or a pinch of hing.
  5. Fry 30 to 60 seconds until fragrant and just crisp at the edges.
  6. Pour over the dish or keep cooking in the same pan.

Do not walk away: leaves go from fragrant to bitter if they burn. Quality leaf matters, see our dried curry leaves quality guide.

Six recipes beyond dal

South Indian lemon rice

After the leaf tadka, add mustard seeds, a dried red chilli and turmeric. Fold through cooked rice, lemon juice, salt and roasted peanuts or fried chana dal. Ready in under 15 minutes with leftover rice.

Quick curry leaf coconut chutney

Soak a generous handful of dried leaves in warm water five minutes, then blend with coconut, green chilli, ginger and tamarind or lime. Temper the finished chutney with mustard seeds and extra leaves in oil. Keeps about a week refrigerated.

Curry leaf tempered eggs

Fry curry leaves with sliced onion in oil until the onion softens. Pour in beaten eggs and scramble. Leaves crisp at the edges and add texture as well as aroma.

Crispy potatoes with curry leaf oil

Roast or pan-fry potatoes until golden. Fry extra leaves in neutral oil, strain if you like, and toss potatoes with that oil, mustard seeds and a pinch of chilli just before serving.

Curry leaf finishing oil

Fry a large handful of dried leaves in oil until crisp, cool, and store in a sealed jar in the fridge up to two weeks. Spoon over soup, plain rice, roasted vegetables or dal when you want instant aroma without a full tadka.

Coconut green beans (poriyal-style)

Steam green beans until just tender. Make a tadka with dried curry leaves, mustard seeds and a dried chilli. Toss beans with the tempering, grated coconut, salt and lemon, about ten minutes, weeknight-friendly.

For a full dal built around the same tempering, see our 30-minute curry leaf dal recipe. For a jar of dry seasoning, try curry leaf podi with dried leaves.

When a recipe lists fresh curry leaves, start with a few more dried leaflets than it asks for and adjust next time.

Fresh or dried curry leaves

Fresh bunches wilt within days and are hit-and-miss outside Indian grocers. Dried leaf keeps for months, fries straight into oil, and rehydrates for chutney. Drying softens the aroma, so plan on a little more dried leaf than fresh, and taste as you go. Out of fresh tonight? See our curry leaf substitute guide.

FAQ

Can I use dried curry leaves without rehydrating them?

Yes for tadka in hot oil. Soak only when blending leaves into a paste, as in chutney.

How long does a 30g pouch last?

With weekday tadka, often one to two months if you reseal after each use and keep the pouch away from steam.

How long does curry leaf oil keep?

About two weeks refrigerated in a sealed jar.

Do dried curry leaves taste as good as fresh?

Close, slightly softer aroma, which is why many cooks use a bit more dried leaf by volume.

What can I substitute if I run out mid-recipe?

There is no perfect swap. Lime zest or kaffir lime leaf can hint at citrus in a pinch. For a full ranked list, see our curry leaf substitute guide.
