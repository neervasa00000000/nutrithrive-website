What it is

Whole dried leaves of the curry tree (Murraya koenigii), the leaf used for tempering across South Indian, Sri Lankan and Gujarati cooking. It isn't curry powder and doesn't taste like it. The leaf has its own warm, citrusy aroma that comes alive in hot fat. Each pouch holds whole leaves, not crushed powder.

How to use

  1. Heat 1 to 2 tbsp oil or ghee until a mustard seed sizzles straight away.
  2. Add mustard or cumin seeds and let them pop.
  3. Add 8 to 15 dried curry leaves for a pot of dal for 3 to 4. They should sizzle and darken within 10 to 20 seconds.
  4. Pour the whole tadka over dal, sambar, rasam, vegies or rice. Recipe written for fresh leaves? Use a little more dried leaf and taste as you go. Avoid cold oil and long frying, which turns them bitter.

Storage and shelf life

Keep the pouch sealed in a cool, dry, dark cupboard, away from the stove. Dried leaves don't spoil quickly when kept dry, but their aroma fades, and a pouch lasts 1 year once opened. Check the best-before date on the pouch.

Frequently asked questions

How many dried curry leaves equal fresh?

There's no exact swap. Drying softens the aroma, so use a little more dried leaf than you would fresh, fry it briefly in hot oil, and taste as you go.

Are curry leaves the same as curry powder?

No. Curry powder is a spice blend (turmeric, coriander, cumin, chilli) and normally contains no curry leaves.

Are curry patta, kadi patta and karipatta the same?

Yes. They're the same leaf in different Indian languages: kadi patta in Hindi, karipatta and karuveppilai in the South, and karapincha in Sinhala.

Do Woolworths and Coles sell curry leaves?

On 5 Oct 2026, Coles listed a 10g fresh punnet, and Woolworths a 5g fresh punnet and a 5g dried pack. Stock varies. See Where to buy curry leaves in Australia.

How should I store dried curry leaves?

Sealed, in a cool, dark cupboard away from steam. Lasts 1 year once opened.
