5 Curry Leaf Recipes Beyond Dal

By Neer, NutriThrive Truganina · Last updated: 09 Aug 2026

Quick Answer

Temper dried curry leaves in hot oil for 30–60 seconds, then use that infused oil for lemon rice, a quick chutney, scrambled eggs, roasted potatoes, or a jar of finishing oil you keep in the fridge.

Curry leaves aren't just for dal. Fried in hot oil at the start of a dish (tadka), they work in rice, eggs, roasted vegetables, and a fast chutney — anywhere a dish could use a citrusy, nutty background note.

Quick summary
  • Curry leaves are tempered (tadka) in hot oil for 30–60 seconds until fragrant and slightly crisp — this step is the same across almost every use
  • Dried leaves need roughly double to triple the quantity of fresh for a similar aroma
  • Five dishes below: lemon rice, curry leaf chutney, tempered eggs, roasted potatoes, and a finishing oil

The one technique behind all five recipes

Heat 1–2 tablespoons of oil or ghee until shimmering, add 8–12 dried curry leaves (they'll crackle), fry 30–60 seconds until fragrant and slightly crisp, then build the rest of the dish around that infused oil. Everything below uses this same starting point.

1. South Indian lemon rice

Temper curry leaves with mustard seeds, a dried red chilli, and a pinch of turmeric in oil. Stir through cooked rice with lemon juice, salt, and a spoon of roasted peanuts or chana dal for crunch. Ready in under 15 minutes and holds well for lunchboxes.

2. Quick curry leaf chutney

Blend a handful of curry leaves (rehydrate dried leaves in warm water for 5 minutes first) with grated coconut, a green chilli, ginger, and a squeeze of tamarind or lime. Temper the finished chutney with mustard seeds and more curry leaves in oil for extra aroma. Keeps in the fridge for about a week.

3. Curry leaf tempered eggs

Fry curry leaves and sliced onion in oil until the onion softens, then scramble eggs directly into the pan. A simple weekday breakfast that's noticeably different from a plain scramble — the leaves crisp up and add texture as well as flavour.

4. Roasted potatoes with curry leaf oil

Toss roasted or pan-fried potatoes with a curry-leaf-infused oil (fry the leaves separately, strain, keep the oil) plus mustard seeds and a pinch of chilli powder just before serving. Works as a side for almost any curry-adjacent meal.

5. Curry leaf finishing oil (keep on hand for everything else)

Fry a generous handful of curry leaves in neutral oil until crisp, cool, and store in a jar in the fridge for up to two weeks. Spoon over soups, dals, roasted vegetables, or plain rice whenever a dish needs a fast flavour boost without starting from scratch.

A note on dried vs fresh

Fresh curry leaves wilt within a few days and aren't widely available outside specialty grocers in most Australian cities. Dried leaf stores for months, rehydrates well for chutneys, and works directly (no soaking needed) for tadka and frying. Use roughly two to three times the amount of dried compared to fresh to match the aroma.

FAQ

Can I use dried curry leaves without rehydrating them?

Yes, for tempering (tadka) in hot oil, dried leaves go in directly — no soaking needed. Rehydrating is only useful for recipes like chutney where you're blending the leaves into a paste.

How long does curry leaf oil keep?

Up to about two weeks refrigerated in a sealed jar.

Do dried curry leaves taste as good as fresh?

Close, though slightly less vibrant in aroma. Using a slightly larger quantity (double to triple) helps close that gap for most recipes.

What can I substitute if I run out of curry leaves mid-recipe?

There's no perfect substitute, but a small amount of lime zest or kaffir lime leaf can approximate the citrus note in a pinch — see our full substitute guide.
