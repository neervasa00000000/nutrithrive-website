When to Stop Caffeine for Better Sleep

By Neer, NutriThrive Truganina · Last updated: 9 Aug 2026

Quick Answer

Most people should stop caffeine 8–10 hours before bed. Caffeine has a half-life of roughly 5–6 hours in an average adult, meaning half of a 2pm coffee is still in your system at 8pm. This is the single biggest reason people fall asleep fine but wake up at 2am wired.

Key Takeaways
  • Caffeine’s half-life is 5–6 hours on average — genetics change this a lot, person to person
  • A 2pm coffee can still leave a quarter of its caffeine in your system by 10pm
  • Switching your last cup to something lower in caffeine (not zero) is often easier to stick to than cutting caffeine cold

Why “I can drink coffee and fall asleep fine” doesn’t mean you’re in the clear

Falling asleep and staying asleep are two different things. Caffeine mainly disrupts deep sleep — the restorative stage — even when it doesn’t stop you nodding off. A lot of people who feel like caffeine “doesn’t affect” their sleep are actually losing sleep quality without noticing it, because they never see the deep-sleep data (unless they’re tracking it).

The half-life math, in plain terms

If you drink a coffee with 95mg of caffeine at 2pm:

  • By 8pm (6 hours later): roughly 47mg still in your system
  • By 2am (12 hours later): roughly 24mg still in your system

That’s why the general guidance from sleep researchers is to stop caffeine 8–10 hours before your target bedtime, not just “don’t drink coffee after lunch” as a vague rule.

What actually happens if you switch your last cup, instead of cutting it

Going from a 3pm coffee (about 95mg) straight to nothing is a hard habit to keep — most people don’t. A lower-caffeine swap is often easier to sustain long-term than an outright cutoff, simply because it doesn’t feel like deprivation.

A simple way to test your own cutoff

Individual caffeine sensitivity varies a lot based on genetics (the CYP1A2 gene, if you want the specific term). Instead of following a generic rule, try this for one week: note your last caffeinated drink’s time and rate your sleep quality (not just whether you fell asleep) the next morning on a 1–5 scale. Move your cutoff 30 minutes earlier each time your average dips, until it stabilises. That’s your actual number, not a guess from an article.

FAQ

Does tea have less caffeine than coffee?

Yes, generally. A cup of black tea like Darjeeling has roughly 40–50mg of caffeine, compared to 80–100mg for a standard coffee — about half.

Is decaf tea completely caffeine-free?

No. Decaf processes remove most, not all, caffeine — usually a few mg remain per cup. True zero-caffeine options are herbal or rooibos blends.

Will one afternoon coffee really affect my sleep if I don’t feel it?

Often yes. Caffeine can reduce deep sleep even when it doesn’t stop you falling asleep or waking you up — which is why people frequently don’t notice the effect without tracking it.

How long before bed should I have my last cup of any caffeinated drink?

A common guideline is 8–10 hours before bedtime, adjusted based on your own sensitivity (see the self-test above).
