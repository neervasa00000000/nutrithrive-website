What’s actually in a moringa leaf (and why skin likes it)

Moringa oleifera leaf is unusually dense in polyphenols and flavonoids — the plant compounds researchers measure when they’re testing antioxidant capacity.

A few things worth knowing, all from published research:

  • A 2023 study in Antioxidants profiled moringa leaf extracts specifically for topical application, and the authors highlighted moringa as a promising ally against oxidative stress in skin. Read the study →
  • Analysis of moringa leaf by HPLC found quercetin as a dominant compound, alongside high concentrations of phenolic and flavonoid compounds. Read the study →
  • Researchers testing moringa extract in skin keratinocyte cells found it supported the cells’ own antioxidant defences against oxidative stress. Read the study →
  • Researchers testing moringa leaf extract as a natural sun-care ingredient found it offered modest UV-filtering properties — roughly SPF 2, around 50% UVB protection, at 2–4% concentration in lab formulations. Read the study →

Two honest notes, because we’d rather you trust us than oversell you:

  1. Most of this research is on concentrated extracts in lab settings, not on a teaspoon of leaf powder mixed at your bathroom sink. A homemade mask is a lovely, gentle ritual — not a clinical treatment.
  2. Moringa is not a sunscreen. Even the strongest lab result above (SPF 2) is far below what’s needed for real protection. In Australia, nothing replaces a broad-spectrum SPF50+. Cancer Council’s sunscreen basics is the guide to follow there.

The 3-Minute Moringa Green Gel (our house recipe)

Most DIY face masks fail for the same reason: they’re made with water. Water makes leaf powder go gritty, then it dries into a cracking clay that pulls at your skin.

So don’t use water. Use aloe vera gel.

Aloe turns moringa powder into a smooth, cool, spreadable green gel that stays soft on your face and rinses off in seconds. This one change is the whole trick.

What you need

IngredientAmount
Moringa powder½ teaspoon
Pure aloe vera gel1 tablespoon
A light oil (jojoba, almond or olive)3–4 drops
Raw honey (optional, for dry skin)¼ teaspoon

How to make it

  1. Spoon the aloe gel into a small bowl or the palm of your hand.
  2. Sift in the moringa. Use the back of a spoon to press out any lumps — takes about 20 seconds.
  3. Stir in the oil (and the honey, if your skin is on the dry side). You want a smooth, deep-green gel with no grit.
  4. Apply to clean, slightly damp skin. Avoid the eye area.
  5. Leave for 8–10 minutes. It should stay soft and cool — it shouldn’t tighten or crack.
  6. Rinse with lukewarm water and pat dry. Follow with your usual moisturiser while skin is still damp.

How often: once or twice a week is plenty. This is a ritual, not a chore.

The smell: fresh, green, faintly like matcha. Nothing perfumed.

One tip that makes a real difference: mix it fresh each time. Moringa’s colour is its freshness indicator — a bright, vivid green means the leaf was shade-dried properly. Dull, khaki-brown powder has been sitting around oxidising, and it won’t feel or smell the same. (If you want the full quality checklist, we wrote one: how to choose moringa powder in Australia.)

The 60-Second Lather Mask (no mixing required)

This is the one almost nobody knows, and it’s our favourite.

If you use a moringa soap bar, you’re probably doing what everyone does: lather, wash, rinse in ten seconds flat. All that plant material goes straight down the drain.

Try this instead:

  1. Wet your face and the bar.
  2. Work up a generous lather in your hands — proper foam, not a film.
  3. Spread it over your face and leave it there for 60 seconds. Breathe. Stretch. Pick your podcast.
  4. Rinse with lukewarm water.

That’s it. Sixty seconds turns a wash into a mini ritual, costs you nothing extra, and it’s the easiest possible entry point if you’ve never made a mask in your life.

Bonus: Moringa Green Ice for hot Aussie mornings

For summer — or any morning your face feels puffy after a late night:

  • Whisk 1 teaspoon of moringa powder into 1 cup of cool water.
  • Pour into an ice cube tray and freeze.
  • Wrap one cube in a thin cotton cloth and glide it over your cheeks, jaw and forehead for 30–60 seconds.

The cold does the de-puffing work; the green tint just makes it feel like a spa. Never press ice directly onto bare skin — always use the cloth.

One tray keeps for about a month in the freezer, and it’s genuinely one of the nicest things you can do for your face on a 38-degree Melbourne day.

Build a simple routine around it

You don’t need ten steps. You need four, and one weekly ritual.

If you have sensitive skin

Sensitive skin does best with less, not more. Cleanse with the moringa bar (60-second lather, twice a week), moisturise while damp, SPF every morning, and use the green gel mask once a week only to start. Skip any scrubbing — moringa leaf is soft, so let the gel do the work.

If you have dry skin

Add the honey to your green gel and bump the oil up to 6 drops. Keep showers short and warm rather than hot. Moisturise within 60 seconds of patting dry — that’s the window where it works hardest. If your dryness is persistent, itchy or inflamed, healthdirect’s guidance on eczema is a good starting point and it’s worth seeing a GP.

If you’re a bloke starting from zero

Genuinely: soap bar, moisturiser, sunscreen. That’s a complete routine. Do the 60-second lather twice a week instead of a normal wash and you’re already ahead of most people. Add the green gel on a Sunday night if you feel like it.

If you have combination skin

Green gel over the whole face, but leave it a couple of minutes longer on your T-zone before rinsing. Twice a week is the sweet spot.

Patch test first — 5 minutes, worth it

Any new ingredient, natural or not, deserves a patch test.

Mix a tiny amount of the gel, dab it on the inside of your forearm or behind your ear, leave for 24 hours, and check for any redness or itching. If all’s clear, you’re good to go.

If you have an existing skin condition, are pregnant, or use prescription actives like retinoids, have a quick chat with your GP or dermatologist first. Frequently asked questions

Can I put moringa powder directly on my face?

Not on its own — dry powder won’t spread and won’t do much. Mix it into a base first. Aloe vera gel is the best option; it keeps the mask soft, cool and easy to rinse. Yoghurt or honey also work well.

Is moringa good for sensitive skin?

Moringa leaf is soft and gentle, and our soap is made without parabens or synthetic fragrance, which are common irritants. That said, everyone’s skin is different — always patch test 24 hours before your first full application.

How often should I use a moringa face mask?

Once or twice a week is plenty. Weekly is the ideal starting point if your skin is sensitive or reactive.

Can I use my moringa soap on my face?

Yes — plenty of our customers use it as a daily face and body wash. For the 60-second lather trick, twice a week is the sweet spot.

Will a moringa mask stain my skin green?

No. It leaves a light green tint while it’s on, and it rinses away completely with lukewarm water. Do keep it off pale towels and white bathmats, though — moringa is a strong pigment on fabric.

Is moringa powder vegan?

Yes. Our moringa powder and moringa soap are both 100% vegan and cruelty-free.

Can I mix moringa into my existing moisturiser?

We’d suggest not — leaf powder doesn’t emulsify into most creams properly and you’ll just get grit. Keep it as a separate weekly mask and use your moisturiser as normal afterwards.

Does moringa replace sunscreen?

No. In Australia, nothing does. Broad-spectrum SPF50+ every day, reapplied every two hours outdoors, per Cancer Council guidance.
