Curry leaves at Coles

Coles stocks fresh curry leaves in some stores, usually in the fresh herb section near the coriander, mint and chillies. It is not a line every Coles carries every week. A large Coles, or a store in an area with a large Indian community, is more likely to stock them regularly.

Dried curry leaves at Coles are even harder to find. Most stores do not carry a dried pouch in the spice aisle.

Check Coles online before you go, and set your store. If it shows fresh curry leaves, buy them the day you plan to cook, because fresh leaves wilt quickly.

Curry leaves at Woolworths

Woolworths is similar. Some stores stock fresh curry leaves in the fresh herb section, usually as a small bunch in a punnet or a bag. Availability varies by store and by season, and it is not a line every Woolworths carries all year.

Dried curry leaves at Woolworths are also hard to find. Woolworths may list them through Everyday Market, sold by a third-party seller, not by Woolworths itself.

Check Woolworths online first and set your store. If you want whole dried curry leaves for tadka, a blend will not do the same job.

Aldi, Indian grocers and online

Aldi does not regularly stock curry leaves. Its range changes with its Special Buys, so it is worth checking, but do not plan a weekly shop around finding curry leaves at Aldi.

Indian grocers are usually the most reliable place to buy fresh curry leaves in Australia. A good grocer in Melbourne, Sydney, Brisbane or Perth will often have fresh leaves, and some also carry dried leaves.

Online, you can buy dried curry leaves from spice sellers and Indian grocers. Check: is it whole dried leaves, or powder? Where is it packed? What is the total delivered price, including shipping?

Fresh vs dried curry leaves

Fresh leaves are bright, citrusy and slightly nutty, and they keep for only a few days in the fridge. Dried leaves have a different, often milder aroma, and they keep for months, sealed, cool and dry. That makes them the everyday choice.

There is no exact fresh-to-dried conversion; start with a small pinch and adjust to taste. Crush the dried leaves lightly before you add them, so they release their flavour. Add them to hot oil at the start of cooking, not at the end.

How to use dried curry leaves

1. Tadka for dal. Heat 1 tablespoon of ghee or oil in a small pan. Add 1 teaspoon of dried curry leaves, 1/2 teaspoon of mustard seeds, and 1 dried red chilli. When the mustard seeds pop and the leaves crisp, pour the hot oil over your cooked dal.

2. Curry leaf rice. Heat oil, add dried curry leaves, mustard seeds, urad dal, peanuts and turmeric. Stir in cooked rice, add salt and lemon juice, and toss for 2 to 3 minutes. For a dry version, see our curry leaf podi recipe with dried curry leaves.

3. Curries and soups. Add 1 teaspoon of dried curry leaves to the oil at the start, with onions, garlic and ginger. They work in vegetable curries, lentil soups, chicken curry and fish curry. Do not add them at the end, they need heat and oil to release their flavour.

If you are out of curry leaves, see our guide to a curry leaves substitute and what to use instead. Bay leaves, lime leaves and lemon zest can help, but none taste quite the same.

How to store dried curry leaves

Store dried curry leaves in a sealed pouch or jar, cool and dry, away from sunlight and steam. Do not store them in the fridge. If they smell dusty or flat when crushed, replace them.

What do curry leaves cost in Australia?

There is no single price for curry leaves in Australia. A fresh bunch is usually a few dollars, when it is in stock. A dried pouch is usually more per pack, but much less per use, because it lasts for months.

Compare price per use, not price per pack. A fresh bunch that wilts in three days can cost more per meal than a dried pouch that lasts for 30 or 40 meals. We do not list a Coles or Woolworths price here, because we did not check a specific supermarket price, and prices change by store and by week.
