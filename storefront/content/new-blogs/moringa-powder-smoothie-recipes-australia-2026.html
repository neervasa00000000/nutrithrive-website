How to hide the taste

Moringa powder tastes green and earthy. On its own in water it is not pleasant. That is normal, and it is fixable. These four things hide the taste best:

  1. Frozen banana. The cold and the sweetness cover the green flavour better than anything else.
  2. Cocoa or cacao. Chocolate flavour is strong enough to carry the moringa with it.
  3. Citrus. Lemon, lime or orange juice lifts the whole smoothie and cuts the earthy taste.
  4. Yoghurt. The creamy, slightly sour taste smooths out the powder.

Also: blend the moringa in properly. Put the powder in with the liquid first and blend for 20 to 30 seconds, so it mixes in without lumps.

The 5 recipes

All five make one large smoothie, about 350 to 400ml, with 1/2 to 1 teaspoon of moringa powder. Use less for a milder flavour; follow the food label.

1. Banana and peanut butter

The best one to start with — it tastes like a normal banana smoothie.

Ingredients

  • 1 frozen banana, about 120g
  • 1 tablespoon peanut butter, about 16g
  • 250ml milk or oat milk
  • 1/2 to 1 teaspoon moringa powder
  • 1 teaspoon honey, optional
  • 4 ice cubes

Steps

  1. Pour the milk in, add the moringa powder, and blend for 10 seconds.
  2. Add the frozen banana, peanut butter, honey and ice, and blend for 30 seconds. Drink straight away.

Why it works: banana and peanut butter are both strong flavours, and the cold banana hides the green taste.

2. Mango and coconut

Sweet, cold and simple.

Ingredients

  • 150g frozen mango pieces
  • 200ml coconut water
  • 50ml coconut milk
  • 1/2 to 1 teaspoon moringa powder
  • Juice of 1/2 lime
  • 4 ice cubes

Steps

  1. Pour the coconut water in, add the moringa powder, and blend for 10 seconds.
  2. Add the mango, coconut milk, lime juice and ice, and blend for 30 seconds.

Why it works: mango is sweet and strong, and the lime cuts the earthy taste. Use frozen mango — the cold helps.

3. Berry and yoghurt

The thickest recipe here, more like a breakfast bowl you can drink.

Ingredients

  • 150g frozen mixed berries
  • 150g Greek yoghurt
  • 150ml milk
  • 1/2 to 1 teaspoon moringa powder
  • 1 teaspoon honey, optional

Steps

  1. Pour the milk in, add the moringa powder, and blend for 10 seconds.
  2. Add the berries, yoghurt and honey, and blend for 30 to 40 seconds until thick.

Why it works: yoghurt and berries are sour and strong, and their colour hides the green powder.

4. Green smoothie: spinach, apple and lemon

Yes, this one is green — and the freshest-tasting, thanks to the lemon.

Ingredients

  • 1 handful baby spinach, about 30g
  • 1 green apple, cored and chopped, about 150g
  • 250ml cold water or coconut water
  • 1/2 lemon, juiced
  • 1/2 to 1 teaspoon moringa powder
  • 1/2 frozen banana, about 60g, optional but recommended

Steps

  1. Pour the water in, add the moringa powder and spinach, and blend for 20 seconds.
  2. Add the apple, lemon juice and frozen banana, and blend for 30 seconds.

Why it works: apple and lemon give it a fresh, sharp taste, and spinach makes the moringa flavour feel normal.

5. Chocolate and banana

For people who do not like green drinks at all — it tastes like chocolate milk.

Ingredients

  • 1 frozen banana, about 120g
  • 1 tablespoon cocoa powder, about 7g
  • 250ml milk
  • 1/2 to 1 teaspoon moringa powder
  • 1 teaspoon honey or maple syrup, optional
  • 4 ice cubes

Steps

  1. Pour the milk in, add the moringa powder and cocoa, and blend for 10 seconds.
  2. Add the frozen banana, honey and ice, and blend for 30 seconds.

Why it works: cocoa is the strongest flavour here and covers the moringa completely.

Blender or shaker?

A blender is better. It mixes the powder in properly, breaks up frozen fruit, and gives a smooth drink with no lumps.

A shaker works if you have no blender, but keep it simple: milk or water, moringa powder, and yoghurt or peanut butter. Shake hard for 30 seconds and drink straight away. Do not use frozen fruit in a shaker, and do not leave it sitting — the powder settles. For travel or the gym, a shaker is fine for one day. At home, use the blender.

Storage: can you make it the night before?

You can, but it is better fresh. If you make one the night before, keep it in a sealed jar in the fridge and drink it within 12 hours. Shake or stir it before drinking, because it will separate overnight.

Do not keep it for two days, do not leave it on the bench, and do not blend a big batch for the whole week — it will go watery and taste flat by day two.

The powder itself keeps well. Keep the pouch sealed, in a cool, dry cupboard, away from light. A 100g pouch gives about 33 measured 3g portions; spoon weight varies. For the full detail, read how long does moringa powder last, shelf life and storage.

Prep tips to make mornings faster

  • Freeze bananas in advance. Peel ripe bananas, break them in half, and freeze them in a bag. They are the base for three recipes here.
  • Make freezer packs. Put the frozen fruit for one smoothie into a small bag, label it, and keep five packs in the freezer. In the morning, tip one pack into the blender, add milk and moringa, and blend.
  • Keep the blender clean. Rinse it straight after blending — dried smoothie is much harder to clean.
  • Buy the fruit frozen. Frozen mango, berries and banana are cheaper than fresh out of season, already cut, and the cold helps hide the moringa taste.

How much moringa powder should I put in a smoothie? The culinary range here is 1/2 to 1 teaspoon, but less is fine. Follow the food label; teaspoons vary in weight and this is not a medical dose.
