Iron deficiency in Australia: why so many people miss it

Iron deficiency can occur with or without anaemia. It often builds slowly, so people get used to feeling tired and put it down to work, kids, or poor sleep.

Women carry most of the burden, because of menstrual blood loss, pregnancy, and often lower dietary intake. Teenage girls, women in their 20s to 40s, pregnant women, and vegetarians and vegans are most likely to run low. Plant iron is non-haem iron and absorbs less well than the haem iron in meat, so vegetarians generally need about 80% more dietary iron than meat-eaters. Food choices, and how you combine them, matter more.

Signs of low iron — and when to see your GP

Common signs are tiredness that does not lift with sleep, pale skin or pale inner eyelids, hair shedding more than usual, brain fog, shortness of breath on mild exertion, headaches, dizziness, cold hands and feet, and brittle nails.

Any one of these can have other causes, which is why a blood test matters. A GP can check ferritin, which shows your iron stores, plus a full blood count. Do not start iron tablets or a daily moringa habit as a treatment without that test — low iron can also signal blood loss, gut problems, or other conditions. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking prescription medication, or managing a health condition, talk to your GP before regular use.

How much iron is in moringa powder, honestly?

Per 100g, moringa leaf is far richer in iron than spinach, lentils, or red meat. Per serve, a teaspoon gives you about the same iron as a small handful of raw spinach, and less than half a cup of cooked lentils. That is still useful — a real top-up from a food you can add to a meal you already eat — but it is not a full day's iron.

Adult women need about 18 mg of iron a day from food for women aged 19–50, and adult men about 8 mg. A daily teaspoon, at 0.84 mg, is roughly 5% of a woman's daily need. Moringa also adds protein (24–27g per 100g dry leaf), vitamin C (46–65 mg per 100g), and calcium (1,443 mg per 100g). Its job is to help close a small gap, every day — not to replace a meal, a blood test, or prescribed iron.

Vitamin C: the part most people skip

Plant iron absorbs poorly on its own, and tea, coffee, and calcium in the same meal can reduce absorption further. Vitamin C in the same meal helps. Moringa leaf itself contains vitamin C, but a teaspoon only gives you about 1.5 to 2 mg — not enough on its own. Pair moringa with orange, mango, capsicum, tomato, lemon, or berries in the same meal. How to use moringa powder for iron

Start with 1/2 teaspoon a day for the first week, then move to 1/2 to 1 teaspoon a day if taste and digestion stay comfortable. Take it with food, not on an empty stomach. There is no established therapeutic dose for iron deficiency.

Try a morning smoothie with 1/2 teaspoon moringa, frozen mango, banana, and oat milk, or stir 1/2 teaspoon into tomato soup, dal, or pasta sauce. Do not take it with tea or coffee, as tannins can reduce plant iron absorption from that meal.

Do not wait for a powder to improve symptoms. Follow your GP’s treatment and blood-test schedule; food powder has not been shown to correct iron deficiency. For more on how women use moringa as part of a normal diet, see our moringa benefits for women guide.

What moringa powder cannot do

It does not treat iron deficiency, anaemia, or low ferritin. If your ferritin is low, you need a GP, a blood test, and a plan — which may include prescribed iron or investigation into why your iron is low. It is not a substitute for prescribed iron tablets, and it does not replace iron-rich meals: lentils, beans, tofu, eggs, red meat, and fortified foods all give you far more iron per serve. If a brand promises to fix anaemia or replace iron tablets, do not buy it.
