What the science says about moringa skin patches

Moringa patches are adhesive products marketed for skin absorption. Brand names that appear in Australian search (including Glorenda and Healrize) change over time, so check each seller's own label and claims. We are not a reseller for those brands. We found no published human trial showing moringa gets through the skin from a patch.

Are moringa patches listed with the TGA?

Some listed medicines in Australia show an AUST L or AUST R number. You can search that number on the ARTG at tga.gov.au. If a moringa patch listing has no AUST L or AUST R number, there is no number on that listing to match on the public register. That tells you the listing did not show a register number. It does not tell you what is inside the patch. Read the ingredient panel, and ask the seller which product a printed number belongs to.

Berberine in moringa patches: what to know

Glorenda lists a "Moringa Berberine 10-in-1" patch. The listing names moringa and berberine together on a skin patch. We have not tested that patch, and we do not sell it. If you take medication, talk with your GP or a pharmacist. Healthdirect can help you find that conversation.

Moringa patch scams: warning signs and how to get a refund

  • Warning signs: countdown timers, "doctor recommended" badges with no names, a "made in" country that changes between the ad and the box, and no Australian address.
  • Before you buy: search the exact shop domain plus "reviews", and pay by card or PayPal, not bank transfer.
  • If the order is wrong or never arrives: ask the seller for a refund in writing, then lodge a chargeback with your bank or a dispute with PayPal, and keep screenshots of the ad and order.
  • Report suspicious ads to Scamwatch (scamwatch.gov.au).
