Glorenda and Healrize patch reviews in Australia: how to read them

Most Australians who land here have typed "Glorenda patches", "Glorenda patch reviews" or "Healrize reviews" (sometimes spelled Glorinda, Glorenda's or Healrise). Some also ask about Clearena. We're a small Melbourne family business that sells moringa leaf powder, so we don't test patches and we won't tell you whether they "work". What we can do is show you how to check a patch listing before you hand over your card details, using the same checks we'd want people to run on us.

Price in dollars and the total landed costThe cart, before paymentPrice in AUD, postage, any international fees, and how many patches are in a pack

ReviewsIndependent review sites, not only the seller's own pageA mix of ratings with dates, not dozens of identical five-star posts on the same day

FAQ

Is Glorenda a legitimate company?

We can't vouch for any patch seller. Check for a business name, an ABN if it's Australian, prices in AUD, a returns address and independent reviews before you buy. The table above lists where to look.

Where is Glorenda sold in Australia?

Listings change. Search the brand name on the Chemist Warehouse site and the brand's own website on the day you buy. We don't sell Glorenda, Healrize or Clearena.

What's the difference between a moringa patch and moringa powder?

A patch is worn on the skin as the brand directs. Leaf powder is a food ingredient you mix into meals and drinks. We sell leaf powder only: one ingredient, packed in Truganina, from $11 for 100g.

How do I spot fake supplement reviews?

Watch for lots of five-star reviews posted on the same few days, reviews with no detail, and ratings that appear only on the seller's own site. Compare with independent review sites and check the returns policy.

Do moringa patches work?

We sell food-use powder, not patches, and do not make medical claims about patch products. Evaluate patch brands on their own evidence and labels.
