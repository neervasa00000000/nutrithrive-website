## Representative competitor review

### The Herbal Connection — broad Australian guide

[The Ultimate Guide to Australian Curry Leaves](https://herbalconnection.com.au/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-australian-curry-leaves-murraya-koenigii/) is a commercial-research hub. It covers leaf vs powder, tempering, uses, fresh vs dried, storage, cultural context, FAQs and a product CTA.

- **Answer speed:** good; the leaf/powder distinction is near the top.
- **Information gain:** moderate; detailed 30–60 second tempering steps and multiple applications.
- **Experience:** asserts product and culinary knowledge, but the research run did not verify a controlled fresh-vs-dried test.
- **Trust:** real business address and product connection; some broad qualitative claims are not independently demonstrated on-page.
- **Format:** long chapter structure, bullets, process steps, FAQs and images.
- **Commercial design:** product links are integrated without replacing the informational answer.
- **Strength:** one coherent overview is easier to navigate than many near-duplicate primers.
- **Weakness:** its breadth makes it less exact for substitute, storage and recipe queries.

### Recipe publishers

- [Waitrose’s curry-leaf recipe collection](https://www.waitrose.com/ecom/recipes/cooks-ingredients/cooks-ingredients-herbs-spices/cooks-ingredients-curry-leaves) provides many recipe choices, search, ratings and time estimates. Its strength is task completion and visual discovery; its weakness for this market is that it is UK-centric and not focused on dried leaves.
- [Coles’ curry-leaf chicken recipe](https://www.coles.com.au/recipes-inspiration/recipes/curry-leaf-chicken) gives servings, prep/cook time, quantities, method and a finished-dish image. Its strength is complete recipe structure and a recognised Australian grocery brand; its weakness is that it answers one dish, not the wider ingredient question.
- Community results contribute genuine usage language and edge cases, but quality and storage claims vary. They are discovery evidence, not a factual source to copy.

## Google-standard interpretation

There is no Google rule that titles must be a specific character count or that descriptions must be a particular length. Google says titles should be unique, descriptive and concise, and snippets are generated mainly from page content with meta descriptions used when useful. Character counts remain preview heuristics, not ranking requirements. Google also recommends crawlable links with descriptive, concise anchor text. Sources: [titles](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link), [snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet), [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide), and [link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable).

## What this research cannot establish

- It cannot prove exact Australian ranks from a general web-search tool.
- It cannot prove cannibalisation without query-by-page GSC exports showing multiple NutriThrive URLs for the same query over time.
- It cannot infer sales, assisted conversions or bounce rate because GA4 landing-page and funnel data were not supplied.
- It cannot validate sensory superiority, shelf-life performance or recipe success without founder evidence.
