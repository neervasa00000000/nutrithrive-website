# Curry-leaf funnel map

Research date: 15 September 2026  
Mode: recommendation only; no funnel, CTA, product, cart or payment changes made.

## Funnel principle

The page must complete the visitor’s immediate job before asking for a purchase. A substitute searcher, recipe cook and dried-leaf buyer are at different stages. Sending every one of them through the same repeated product block creates a technically connected funnel but not necessarily a persuasive one.

## Recommended future pathways

### Transactional

`buy dried curry leaves Australia` → **PDP** → add to cart → recipes / storage after purchase

The PDP should receive transaction-ready visits directly. The broad guide should not intercept this query with a second quasi-PDP.

### Ingredient emergency

`curry leaf substitute` → **substitute guide** → honest swap/omit decision → curry-vs-powder clarification or PDP

The commercial offer is relevant, but only after the page answers what can be done right now.

### Format objection

`fresh vs dried curry leaves` → **fresh-vs-dried guide** → storage or PDP

Real side-by-side evidence would reduce doubt more effectively than more sales copy. **FOUNDER ASSET REQUIRED**.

### Cooking inspiration

`how to use curry leaves` → **ways-to-use page** → complete recipes → PDP

`curry leaf recipes` → **complete recipe collection** → PDP → storage

These two routes should cross-link but not collapse into the same page.

### Product support and retention

PDP/order → **storage** → **ways to use / complete recipes** → repeat purchase

This is the cluster’s clearest retention path. It reduces unused product and gives the $7 pack a job beyond one dish.

### Trust

PDP or broad guide → **narrowed farm/tradition story** → PDP

Only real, attributable production evidence belongs here. Farm, harvest, drying and packing photography must come from the founder; do not generate imaginary documentary proof.

## Commercial-pressure check

The local HTML contains 8–14 links to the curry PDP on most articles, often because templates repeat the same product, footer and shop modules. The master audit marks the current blog-to-product funnels GOOD, but link count is not a conversion metric. Future work should improve placement and relevance, not add more links.

- **GOOD:** one contextual product bridge after the answer, plus a related-product component.
- **WEAK:** generic product blocks that do not explain why the product solves this page’s problem.
- **TOO_AGGRESSIVE:** repeated product CTAs on health/claim-sensitive pages or a broad guide that behaves like a duplicate PDP.
- **MISSING:** GA4 evidence showing which blog landings actually create product views, add-to-cart events and purchases.

## Measurement plan for any future change

Measure per landing URL and query family:

1. organic impressions, selected NutriThrive URL, average position and CTR;
2. engaged-session rate and useful scroll depth;
3. contextual blog-to-PDP click;
4. PDP view, add to cart, checkout start and purchase;
5. assisted conversion and repeat purchase for storage/recipe journeys.

Do not call a content change successful from rankings alone. Do not call it unsuccessful from one week of volatile data.
