# Curry Leaf Podi Recipe (With Dried Curry Leaves)

*A 15-minute South Indian curry leaf podi made with dried curry leaves. Exact ratios, how to store it, and six ways to use it beyond rice.*

![Three spoons holding ground spices and salt, with chillies and cumin seeds on a pale surface](/assets/images/blog/curry-leaf-podi-spices.webp)

*Photo: Unsplash / Chad Montano. The image shows assorted spices, not the finished podi.*

Curry leaf podi is a dry, roasted South Indian spice and lentil powder. Stir it into hot rice with ghee or sesame oil, or serve it with idli and dosa. In Tamil, it is called *karuveppilai podi*.

Dried curry leaves are a practical choice here: podi needs crisp leaves before grinding, and dried leaves need only a brief, gentle toast to release their aroma. Fresh leaves need more time in the pan to shed their moisture.

## What curry leaf podi is

*Podi* means powder. South Indian kitchens make several kinds, including chilli and lentil *milagai podi* and leaf-led curry leaf podi. Both are dry chutney powders, but curry leaf podi has a more herbal flavour. It is different from wet coconut chutney.

Curry powder is also a different ingredient. It is a blend of spices, often including turmeric, coriander and cumin; curry leaves are not necessarily in it. See our [curry leaves versus curry powder guide](/blog/curry-leaves-vs-curry-powder-difference-explained-2026) for the full comparison.

## Why use dried curry leaves?

This batch uses **10 g dried curry leaves**. Weigh the leaves rather than measuring a loose handful: whole and broken leaves fill a cup differently. Toast them on low heat for about 1–2 minutes, just until fragrant and crisp. They can scorch quickly.

For tempering, cooks may use more dried than fresh leaf by volume. That volume swap does not give a reliable ratio for a ground podi. Read our [dried curry leaves quality guide](/blog/dried-curry-leaves-quality-guide-how-to-use) for more preparation tips.

## Curry leaf podi recipe

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Dried curry leaves | 10 g |
| Chana dal | 2 tbsp |
| Urad dal | 2 tbsp |
| Dried red chillies | 4–6, or 2 for a milder batch |
| Cumin seeds | 1 tsp |
| Black peppercorns | ½ tsp |
| White sesame seeds, optional | 1 tbsp |
| Tamarind, optional | Small piece or ¼ tsp tamarind powder |
| Jaggery, optional | ¼ tsp |
| Asafoetida (hing) | Pinch |
| Salt | About ½ tsp, then adjust |

**Active time:** About 15 minutes. **Cooling:** About 10 minutes. Store the finished batch in an airtight jar; the amount it fills depends on how coarsely you grind it.

### Method

1. **Roast the dals.** Put a heavy pan over low heat. Add the chana dal and urad dal. Dry-roast, stirring often, until golden and nutty, about 5–7 minutes.
2. **Add the spices.** Add the dried chillies, cumin and peppercorns. Roast for about 1 minute, until fragrant.
3. **Toast the sesame, if using.** Stir it in for about 30 seconds, until it starts to pop and deepen in colour.
4. **Add the leaves last.** Stir in the dried curry leaves for 1–2 minutes, until fragrant and crisp. Remove the pan from the heat before the leaves scorch.
5. **Bloom the hing off the heat.** Sprinkle it into the warm pan and stir once.
6. **Cool completely.** Tip the mixture onto a plate. Warm ingredients can steam in the grinder and make the powder clump.
7. **Grind.** Pulse with the salt, tamarind and jaggery, if using, to a coarse, sandy texture. Taste and adjust the salt.

## Six ways to use podi beyond rice

1. **With idli or dosa:** Mix a little podi with oil for dipping, or sprinkle it over the top.
2. **On eggs or avocado toast:** Add a pinch just before serving.
3. **On roasted vegetables:** Toss cooked potatoes or cauliflower with a little podi near the end of roasting.
4. **In a yoghurt dip:** Stir ½–1 tsp into thick plain yoghurt and serve with cucumber or roti.
5. **Over dal:** Finish a bowl with a teaspoon of podi. Try it with our [curry leaf dal recipe](/blog/curry-leaves-dahl-recipe-30-minutes-australia-2026).
6. **On lentil or vegetable soup:** Sprinkle a little over each bowl just before serving, so the roasted aroma stays noticeable.

The classic serving is still hot rice with 1–2 teaspoons of podi and a spoon of ghee or sesame oil. For more ideas, see [curry leaf recipes beyond dal](/blog/curry-leaves-recipes-beyond-dal).

## How to store homemade podi

Keep it in an airtight jar in a cool, dry place. Always use a clean, dry spoon. The roasted aroma is best in the first few weeks and gradually fades. Discard it if it becomes damp or shows signs of spoilage. Our [dried curry leaves guide](/blog/dried-curry-leaves-australia-guide) has more dry-jar tips.

## Allergen and diet notes

Sesame is an allergen; leave the seeds out if someone eating the podi has a sesame allergy. Some compounded hing contains wheat flour, so check its label if you need a gluten-free batch. Use oil instead of ghee for a vegan serving.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I make curry leaf podi with fresh leaves?

Yes. Wash and dry them thoroughly, then gently roast them until crisp enough to crumble before grinding. Dried leaves skip most of that moisture-removal step.

### How long does homemade podi last?

It is most aromatic in the first few weeks when kept dry in an airtight jar. Check for moisture or spoilage rather than relying on a fixed shelf-life promise.

### Is it spicy?

The chilli amount sets the heat. Start with two chillies for a milder batch; add more for a hotter one.

### Can I make it without lentils?

You can make a leaf and sesame or leaf and peanut version, but that changes the texture and allergen profile. Test a small batch before replacing the dal in this recipe.

### Is podi the same as “gunpowder”?

Not exactly. *Milagai podi*, often called gunpowder, is chilli and lentil led. Curry leaf podi is leaf led and more herbal.
